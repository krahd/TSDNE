# TSDNE audit — 7 August 2026

**Audited head:** `47fb889035ed6dbe06a6b422dd5c51779e426091`  
**Scope:** expanded 39-family generator, structure/colour/finish separation, candidate selection, responsiveness changes, public-project framing, private 250-run curation instrument, and paper implications.

## Overall judgement

The 7 August revision is a substantial improvement. The expansion from twelve to thirty-nine structural families, together with independent colour and finish axes, directly addresses the earlier risk that repeated encounters would expose a shallow or recognisable house style. Relaxing the previous complexity enrichment is also correct: forcing density, turbulence, depth, and complexity upwards made the generator systematically busy and reduced contrast between encounters.

The revision does not change the conceptual centre of TSDNE. The public interaction remains one shader at a time, with no prompt, preference learning, visible optimisation process, or model runtime. The expanded generator should therefore be treated as infrastructure required to make computational abundance experientially credible, not as the work's principal originality claim.

No critical conceptual regression was found. Four implementation/research issues should be resolved before the 250-run study is treated as evidence.

## Finding 1 — candidate pools are category-biased by the ordered family schedule

**Severity:** high for curation evidence; medium for the public artwork.

`FAMILY_NAMES` is deliberately grouped by category: continuous fields, constructivist/hard-edge geometry, reprographic systems, recursive/fractal systems, marched space, biological systems, and physical systems.

`familySchedule()` removes recently used families, selects a random starting offset, and then walks the remaining array sequentially. `buildCandidatePool()` stops as soon as it has five valid candidates for the initial encounter or nine valid candidates thereafter.

The consequence is that a candidate pool usually samples a contiguous neighbourhood of the categorically ordered list rather than the full 39-family vocabulary. A random offset changes where the neighbourhood begins but does not remove this categorical clustering.

This matters in two ways:

1. the public selector may compare several closely related structural families in one request while not considering distant categories at all;
2. the private curation study would measure the distribution induced by this schedule rather than an approximately balanced sample of the generator.

### Recommended correction

Before the curation study, replace rotation-through-an-ordered-list with one of:

- a Fisher–Yates shuffle of eligible families for every pool;
- a stratified schedule drawing across macro-categories before repeating a category;
- or, preferably for the study, an explicit balanced design that targets roughly equal observations per family/category independently of the public selector.

Do not silently use the curation data to estimate family quality until this sampling issue is resolved or explicitly modelled.

## Finding 2 — the 250-run curation branch evaluates the obsolete 12-family generator

**Severity:** critical for the planned study.

The private research branch `research/curation-instrument-250` was created from commit `261010160d93dd495bdc5448ea59b094fe41a9d2`, before the 39-family expansion. Its current head is `071e72fd2fe58f4ffd5835fe8a79ca8e79ecbdf5`.

The instrument itself remains useful: it records explicit `interesting`, `not-interesting`, and `skip` judgements, optional comments, reconstructive graph/seed data, perceptual descriptors, selector metrics, candidate-pool summaries, generation conditions, and viewing duration. But it must not be used for the planned 250 observations until it is refreshed against the current generator and selector.

### Recommended correction

Refresh or recreate the research branch from current `main`, then adapt its schema to the new generator fields:

- `colorMode`;
- `finish`;
- `variant`;
- expanded 39-family vocabulary;
- colour-mode selection score;
- any macro-category identifier added to address Finding 1.

The analysis script must also include these fields and should report counts by macro-category, family, colour mode, and finish.

## Finding 3 — CI does not actually WebGL-compile every generated family

**Severity:** medium/high until browser validation is complete.

Current generator tests verify that every family:

- emits deterministic GLSL text;
- contains expected declarations and `main`;
- balances braces and parentheses;
- routes colour through `shade()` and `finish()`;
- stays within the estimated cost budget.

This is useful static coverage, but `compileGraph()` is a source emitter; the test named `all authored visual families compile deterministically` does not invoke a GLSL compiler or WebGL driver. A syntactically balanced shader can still fail real GLSL ES 3.00 compilation or behave differently across drivers.

The successful GitHub Pages deployment and JavaScript CI therefore do not prove that all 39 families compile and render on actual browsers.

### Recommended correction

Add an empirical/browser test before publication claims about the full family set:

1. instantiate a WebGL2 context;
2. compile at least one fixed seed for every family × colour mode × finish class where practical;
3. record compile/link failures and renderer information;
4. run a smaller cross-browser matrix on Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Android, and iOS;
5. retain screenshots or low-resolution frame signatures for regression checks.

For the 250-run curation study, log any generator or compile rejection separately rather than allowing failed families to disappear invisibly from the sampled distribution.

## Finding 4 — clearing local anti-repetition memory leaves session colour memory active

**Severity:** low/medium.

The revised selector deliberately keeps `recentModes` only in RAM so colour-mode history is not added to the persistent anti-archive. This is conceptually coherent.

However, the **clear local anti-repetition memory** operation clears IndexedDB traces and resets `recentTraces` and `recentFamilies`, but does not reset `recentModes`. The interface therefore says the local anti-repetition memory has been cleared while one component of the current session's anti-repetition state continues to affect selection.

### Recommended correction

Reset `recentModes = []` when the visitor clears anti-repetition memory, or change the interface wording if session-only colour separation is intentionally excluded from the clear operation.

## Finding 5 — the new named visual references need an explicit genealogy

**Severity:** medium for scholarly/public documentation, not a runtime defect.

The new grammar includes names such as `richter-rhythm`, `eggeling-diagonal`, `suprematist-drift`, and `bauhaus-tiles`. These are useful because they make an authored visual genealogy explicit rather than hiding stylistic sources inside an opaque learned corpus. But the project documentation should explain the references rather than leave them as unexplained style labels.

Hans Richter's *Rhythmus 21/23* and Viking Eggeling's *Symphonie diagonale* belong to the early abstract/absolute-film tradition; MoMA describes their use of abstract forms animated over time. Suprematism, Constructivism, and Bauhaus are established genealogies of geometric abstraction. These are historical references, not claims that the procedural families reproduce particular works.

### Recommended documentation

Add a visual-grammar genealogy identifying:

- historical references and what formal problem each contributes;
- procedural/computational precedents such as Truchet tiling, Julia sets, Apollonian packing, KIFS, ray marching, reaction/dynamical fields, phyllotaxis, contour mapping, and reprographic techniques;
- the distinction between explicit reference and stylistic imitation;
- the fact that the public runtime uses no learned corpus.

Primary museum sources checked during this audit:

- Museum of Modern Art, Hans Richter, *Rhythmus 21* and *Rhythmus 23*: https://www.moma.org/collection/works/309358 and https://www.moma.org/collection/works/318339
- The Metropolitan Museum of Art, “Geometric Abstraction”: https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/geometric-abstraction

## Finding 6 — runtime model refusal and AI-assisted development must remain distinct

**Severity:** medium for the paper and public claims.

The latest generator expansion is explicitly committed with `Co-Authored-By: Claude Opus 5`. The existing `docs/AI-USE-STATEMENT.md` already says that contemporary programming assistants may be used during development, while no model runs in the public artwork.

That distinction should be retained and made more concrete in the production history:

- **runtime claim:** no LLM, diffusion model, remote inference service, learned generative checkpoint, or model host is required to generate visitor encounters;
- **development fact:** AI programming assistants have contributed code and documentation under the artist's direction and review.

The paper should not use “authored procedural system” as shorthand for “written without AI assistance.” Here “authored” should mean that the generative rules, accepted code, visual grammar, curation, and responsibility are explicit and reviewable rather than inferred at runtime from an opaque training corpus.

## Finding 7 — the new three-axis grammar strengthens the paper but should not become a novelty claim

**Severity:** conceptual guidance.

The strongest technical change is the separation of:

1. structural family;
2. colour mode;
3. reprographic finish.

This is important because perceived abundance depends on more than the number of named families. It gives the paper a concrete account of how the work attempts to sustain heterogeneous encounters without profiling the visitor.

It should not be presented as a new procedural-graphics technique. Its scholarly role is evidentiary: it operationalises the requirement that the work's claim of abundance survive repeated encounters.

## Curation-study implications

The 250-run study now becomes more valuable, but its protocol must change.

### Recommended sampling unit

For each reviewed shader record:

- macro-category;
- family;
- colour mode;
- finish;
- complete traits and palette;
- seed and generator version;
- selector score components;
- candidate pool composition;
- perceptual descriptors;
- explicit interesting/not-interesting/skip judgement;
- optional comment;
- technical failures or rejections before selection.

### Recommended design

The study should have two distinguishable phases:

**A. balanced generator audit** — deliberately sample the expanded grammar across categories/families rather than merely rating what the public selector happens to choose.

**B. selector audit** — separately rate outputs chosen by the actual public selector and compare them with alternative candidates in each logged pool.

This separation will let us determine whether a poor result is caused by:

- the family itself;
- a parameter region;
- colour/finish interaction;
- a technical quality threshold;
- or the selector preferring the wrong candidate.

The resulting material can support the practice-led paper as transparent production evidence. A separate methods paper would require a stronger formal result and held-out validation.

## Paper implications

The paper should now explicitly describe:

- perceived abundance as an engineering/aesthetic requirement rather than a rhetorical assertion;
- the move from 12 to 39 structural families after early visual-range criticism;
- separation of structure, colour, and surface finish;
- the removal of the previous maximal-complexity bias;
- the private artist-only curation instrument as development methodology, clearly separated from visitor profiling;
- the distinction between no-model runtime and AI-assisted software development;
- explicit visual/art-historical genealogy as an alternative to opaque corpus provenance;
- the limitations of current perceptual metrics and the need for real-browser and human visual validation.
