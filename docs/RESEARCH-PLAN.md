# Research plan

## Primary research object

The primary object is the encounter architecture, not the shader-generation technique in isolation.

## Phase 1 — artistic validation

### Visual corpus

Generate at least 250 outputs across all families and record:

- compilation pass rate;
- quality-screen rejection reasons;
- family frequency;
- family-specific visual weaknesses;
- approximate perceptual coverage;
- examples of near-repetition;
- examples of complexity becoming noise;
- examples of quiet but articulated outputs.

### Sequence validation

Test repeated sessions rather than isolated images:

- three reloads;
- first four encounters;
- ten **another** actions;
- return after one day with persisted local traces;
- return after clearing local traces.

The central criterion is whether the system sustains the experience of consequential uncertainty rather than exposing a finite set of templates.

## Phase 2 — interaction study

A formal participant study is optional and should not be added merely for venue legibility.

Possible questions:

- Do visitors understand **another** as replacement, exploration, rejection, or refresh?
- Does the absence of a catalogue increase attention or merely frustrate?
- Does **take the code** feel like acquisition, continuation, appropriation, or technical utility?
- Do visitors interpret the technical manifest as a certificate despite its refusal?
- Does the explanation of local anti-repetition memory change trust or experience?
- Does the phrase “does not exist” invoke AI, uniqueness, potentiality, or fabrication?

Methods could include observation, short interviews, and post-encounter reflection. No covert analytics should be introduced.

## Phase 3 — provenance analysis

Examine the distinction between:

- internal identity;
- public certification;
- reconstructability;
- ownership;
- authenticity;
- title;
- market scarcity.

The technical manifest provides a concrete object for analysis while explicitly refusing certificate status.

## Phase 4 — anti-repetition analysis

Document the local trace mechanism as an anti-archive:

- quantify reduction in near-repetition;
- test descriptor collisions;
- measure storage size;
- verify non-reconstructability;
- compare no-history, session-history, and persistent-local-history conditions;
- demonstrate that the same mechanism is applied independently of visitor action.

The objective is not to claim a new novelty-search algorithm. It is to show how a modest technical mechanism supports non-extractive individualisation.

## Phase 5 — model refusal

Situate TSDNE in relation to:

- “this person does not exist” and related synthetic-media projects;
- procedural and generative art;
- AI-art infrastructures;
- browser-native computational artworks;
- anti-copyright and free-culture practices;
- NFT and provenance-market aesthetics;
- recommender systems and behavioural personalisation;
- preservation of software-based art.

The model refusal must be argued as a situated artistic decision, not a universal condemnation of machine learning.

## Secondary technical work

The archived WebLLM branch may support a separate study of model mutation, intention–action–effect fidelity, or small-model program control. Such work should not be allowed to redesign TSDNE’s public interaction.

## Outputs

1. complete public artwork;
2. source release and archived version;
3. practice-led journal article;
4. exhibition dossier;
5. technical note on local anti-repetition memory;
6. optional separate model-behaviour study using the archived branch.
