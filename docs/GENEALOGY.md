# Genealogy of *This Shader Does Not Exist*

**Status:** verified research genealogy  
**Reference date:** 4 August 2026  
**Scope:** art, media history, human–computer interaction, computer graphics, shader systems, procedural and evolutionary computation, artificial intelligence, browser-local inference, software studies, political economy, accessibility, security, preservation, and adjacent implementation practice.

## 1. Purpose and method

This document positions *This Shader Does Not Exist* (TSDNE) within the histories that materially precede it. It is not a list of works that merely use shaders, generative systems, browsers, or artificial intelligence. An item is included when it helps explain at least one constitutive feature of TSDNE:

- a browser encounter organised around one generated object at a time;
- replacement or refresh as an irreversible decision;
- procedurally or computationally generated moving images;
- executable source as the transferable object;
- typed or modular shader synthesis;
- artificial intelligence acting inside a bounded artistic ontology;
- local rather than server-side inference;
- individualisation without behavioural profiling;
- relation without exclusive ownership;
- apparent abundance combined with interface-produced scarcity;
- local novelty management without a false claim of global uniqueness;
- browser-specific questions of compatibility, security, accessibility, preservation, and material cost.

“Genealogy” is used rather than “prior-art search” because the relevant relationships are heterogeneous. Some works are close formal precedents; some establish technical methods; some provide conceptual oppositions; others identify risks or limits. Similarity does not imply derivation, and the absence of a discovered exact predecessor does not establish priority.

### Source practice

Titles, creators, dates, operating principles, and technical claims were checked against original papers, artist or project sites, repositories, standards, publisher records, or institutional collections whenever possible. Secondary sources are used only when reception is itself relevant or no stronger record was available. Items whose identity or operating principle could not be verified are excluded and listed in the separate verification log.

The `Source basis` column below names the record on which each close comparison rests. It is not a claim that every negative comparison can be proved exhaustively; it identifies the evidence used to describe the precedent.

## 2. TSDNE in one sentence

TSDNE is a browser artwork in which a visitor encounters one continuously animated, computationally generated shader, may irreversibly relinquish it for another, or may take its freely reusable executable source; generation is immediate and local, while a small optional in-browser language model may propose bounded mutations that deterministic code validates.

That absence is not, by itself, a substantial novelty claim: almost any sufficiently detailed conjunction can be made unique. TSDNE’s defensible argument is instead that the constraints are **mutually motivated refusals**. Each answers a specific counter-model, and each changes the meaning of the others.

### 3.1 Necessity rather than conjunctive novelty

| TSDNE constraint | Counter-model or inherited convention | Function of the refusal |
|---|---|---|
| One generated object at a time | ShaderToy, Artbreeder, galleries, feeds, and parameter studios | Removes comparison, ranking, preview, and catalogue browsing; makes attention temporal |
| Irreversible **another** | Browser history, saved seeds, persistent branches, archive interfaces | Makes search consequential and produces the possibility of regret |
| Transferable executable source | “This X Does Not Exist” sites that deliver only images; platform-bound AI output | Makes the process, not merely its surface, available for appropriation and continuation |
| No project-controlled behavioural profile | Recommender systems, accounts, analytics, taste optimisation | Allows the encounter to become personal through experience rather than personalised in advance |
| Optional local bounded model | Cloud shader assistants and general-purpose prompt services | Makes model agency explicit, narrow, defeasible, and non-essential to first contact |
| No proprietary scarcity | Art Blocks, NFTs, editions, canonical private ownership | Separates singular encounter from exclusive title, artificial rarity, and resale |
| No public archive | ShaderToy, Artbreeder, Picbreeder, Electric Sheep | Refuses accumulation as the default measure of value while preserving voluntary outward circulation |
| Bounded local history | Global registries and hidden cross-user tracking | Uses memory only to reduce repetition on one device, without constructing a social identity |

The project should therefore be argued as a coherent political and aesthetic construction, not as the first occurrence of six independently familiar components.

## 4. Chronological genealogy

### 4.1 Algorithmic and rule-based art before browsers

#### Early computer and algorithmic art: Nake, Molnár, Nees, Mohr, and related pioneers

The 1960s established that an artwork may be constituted by an algorithm and a field of possible outputs rather than a single manually fixed image. Frieder Nake’s plotter works, Vera Molnár’s combinatorial and computer-generated drawings, Georg Nees’s programmed graphics, Manfred Mohr’s algorithmic structures, and related practices are fundamental ancestors of every generative system in TSDNE’s lineage.

Their relevance is not that they resemble contemporary fragment shaders. It lies in four propositions:

1. an authored rule system may be the operative artwork;
2. execution produces instances rather than copies of a fixed original;
3. controlled randomness and permutation can be compositional materials;
4. the artist authors a possibility space and its constraints.

TSDNE inherits this ontology but changes the social interface. The visitor does not see a series, edition, plotter archive, or parameter panel. The work compresses the possibility space into a sequence of singular, non-recoverable encounters.

**Sources:** Digital Art Museum, “The Pioneering Years”; ZKM artist and collection records for Frieder Nake and Vera Molnár.

#### Sol LeWitt and instruction-based art

LeWitt’s proposition that the idea can function as the machine that makes the art remains a conceptual ancestor of software art. TSDNE differs because its instructions are not merely executable by human fabricators: they run continuously on a visitor’s machine, produce perceptually unstable time-based output, and can themselves be transferred as source.

LeWitt is therefore a conceptual rather than technical precedent. The important inheritance is the separation between the generative rule and its instantiations.

#### Harold Cohen, AARON (late 1960s/1973–2016)

AARON is an indispensable AI-art precedent. Cohen developed a rule-based program capable of producing drawings and paintings through a long-term, artist-authored representational ontology. Its historical importance to TSDNE lies in its refusal of a universal generative service: AARON is a bespoke artistic system whose capacities and limits were shaped through decades of artistic work.

TSDNE’s optional local model is closer to this notion of **narrow, authored artificial agency** than to a general chatbot. However, TSDNE distributes agency differently:

- authored typed graph and visual families define what can exist;
- procedural generation produces immediate candidates;
- an LLM may propose only bounded mutations;
- validators decide what may execute;
- the visitor decides whether to remain, relinquish, or take the code.

**Sources:** Whitney Museum, *Harold Cohen: AARON*; Digital Art Museum, Harold Cohen/AARON records.

### 4.2 Artificial evolution and open-ended image spaces

#### Karl Sims, *Artificial Evolution for Computer Graphics* (1991)

Sims represented procedures as symbolic expressions and used evolutionary operations plus human selection to generate images, textures, and animations. This is one of the clearest technical ancestors of typed procedural shader evolution.

The work establishes:

- program trees as visual genotypes;
- mutation and recombination of executable representations;
- human aesthetic judgement as a fitness function;
- the visual design space as something explored rather than directly authored;
- dynamic imagery as the result of compact executable structures.

TSDNE adopts the program-space insight while refusing explicit optimisation by the visitor. The visitor sees no population and selects no preferred parent; replacing the current work is a wager rather than a comparison among options.

**Source:** Karl Sims, “Artificial Evolution for Computer Graphics,” SIGGRAPH 1991.

#### Interactive evolutionary computation

Takagi’s 2001 survey formalised a broad field in which human evaluation supplies fitness for evolutionary search. IEC explains Sims, Picbreeder, procedural shader evolution, and later LLM-assisted shader mutation as members of a common family.

The HCI problem is evaluation fatigue: repeated ranking is demanding and can collapse exploration toward familiar preferences. TSDNE’s design avoids explicit ratings. Dwell, refresh, export, and model invocation may become weak interaction signals, but they are semantically ambiguous and should not be silently interpreted as liking.

**Source:** Hideyuki Takagi, “Interactive Evolutionary Computation,” *Proceedings of the IEEE* 89(9), 2001, DOI 10.1109/5.949485.

#### Andrej Bauer, *Random Art*

*Random Art* generates expression trees that describe images. Its importance lies in typed compositional validity, reproducibility, and the ability to derive complex visual outcomes from symbolic structures. Bauer’s later browser implementation also documents the preservation problem of software dependencies and “rotten software.”

TSDNE inherits the typed-expression principle but adds temporal output, local novelty comparison, constrained model intervention, and irreversible encounter.

**Sources:** Andrej Bauer, “Random Art in Python”; “Random Art and the Law of Rotten Software.”

#### Picbreeder (2008–2011)

Picbreeder enabled visitors to evolve images collaboratively. Images were stored as mathematical objects with persistent lineages that others could branch and continue. It demonstrated that user-guided evolutionary systems can discover complex images through stepping stones not anticipated by an objective function.

Picbreeder is simultaneously close and opposite to TSDNE:

- both concern exploration of large generative spaces;
- both make executable structures into aesthetic objects;
- Picbreeder shows populations, preserves ancestry, and rewards collective continuation;
- TSDNE shows one object, suppresses the public archive, and makes relinquishment irreversible.

**Sources:** Secretan et al., “Picbreeder: A Case Study in Collaborative Evolutionary Exploration of Design Space,” *Evolutionary Computation* 19(3), 2011, DOI 10.1162/EVCO_a_00030; Picbreeder project.

#### Ganbreeder and Artbreeder (2018–)

Joel Simon’s Ganbreeder, subsequently Artbreeder, is the browser successor most directly indebted to Picbreeder. It treats images as branchable and mixable objects, exposes latent parameters, permits public sharing, and turns collaborative browsing into exploration of a model’s possibility space. The official project account explicitly names Picbreeder as its conceptual ancestor.

Artbreeder is important precisely because it clarifies what TSDNE refuses: visible controls, persistent images, public profiles, branching ancestry, remixability inside the platform, and a large searchable community corpus. TSDNE retains exploratory generation but removes the population, genealogy, and platform archive from the immediate encounter.

**Sources:** Joel Simon, Ganbreeder project history; Artbreeder, “About.”

#### LLuminate (2025–)

Joel Simon’s LLuminate is an evolutionary computation framework for generative-AI artefacts. Its implementation supports prompt-defined experiments, populations, crossover, creative strategies, novelty-oriented selection, reproducible seeds, stored generations, and shader rendering. It is a particularly close technical precedent for LLM-mediated mutation of executable visual artefacts.

Its relation to TSDNE is nevertheless methodological rather than experiential. LLuminate is an experimental framework that makes populations and iterative optimisation explicit; TSDNE conceals the candidate population, does not ask the visitor to optimise, and constrains the model to a subordinate role inside an encounter whose first image is procedural.

**Source:** Joel Simon, `joel-simon/lluminate` repository.

#### Novelty search and MAP-Elites

Novelty search replaces objective fitness with behavioural difference. MAP-Elites maps diverse high-performing solutions across user-defined feature dimensions. These methods support TSDNE’s plural descriptors, family coverage, and rejection of recent repetition. They do not justify a universal beauty function.

Appropriate roles are limited:

- avoiding immediate structural repetition;
- maintaining coverage across visual families;
- rejecting blank, clipped, static, or trivial failures;
- selecting from several valid candidates without reducing all values to one score.

**Sources:** Lehman and Stanley, “Abandoning Objectives,” DOI 10.1162/EVCO_a_00025; Mouret and Clune, “Illuminating Search Spaces by Mapping Elites,” arXiv:1504.04909; Woolley and Stanley, novelty-assisted interactive evolution.

#### *Electric Sheep* (1999–)

*Electric Sheep* combines distributed generative animation, evolutionary selection, open-source software, and audience voting. It supplies a major ancestor for inexhaustible animated visual culture.

Its networked collective archive and later collectable forms are decisive differences: TSDNE keeps production local, withholds a public corpus, and refuses editioned scarcity. *Electric Sheep* demonstrates that endless computational supply can still generate social selection, canonical objects, and markets; TSDNE asks what remains when those structures are removed.

**Sources:** Electric Sheep official site and repository.

### 4.3 Demoscene intros and executable graphics

The demoscene is not a peripheral analogy but a direct historical culture of executable visual art. In 4k and 64k intros, a complete real-time audiovisual work is distributed as a small executable program. Because image, geometry, texture, motion, and sound cannot be stored conventionally within the size limit, they are generated at runtime from compact procedures; contemporary productions frequently rely heavily on GPU shaders.

This lineage contributes several points that browser-shader histories alone obscure:

- executable code is itself the portable artwork;
- procedural generation is a material response to distribution constraints;
- technical virtuosity and aesthetic composition are inseparable;
- platform specificity and hardware compatibility are part of reception;
- source may be hidden even when the executable is freely circulated;
- compression, minification, and runtime synthesis shape form.

TSDNE differs from a demo or intro because each exported shader is one member of an encounter-generated family rather than a fixed authored production. It also replaces scene competition and downloadable canonical binaries with non-exclusive code appropriation. Nevertheless, any graphics-facing paper must acknowledge 4k/64k intros and executable graphics as major ancestors of shader-as-artwork.

**Sources:** Markku Reunanen, “Four Kilobyte Art,” *WiderScreen* 17(1–2), 2014; scene.org PC Demoscene FAQ; ACM SIGGRAPH, “Demoscene: The Underground Art of Real-time,” 2019; executable.graphics.

### 4.4 Exhaustive and inexhaustible software artworks

#### John F. Simon Jr., *Every Icon* (1997)

*Every Icon* attempts to display every black-and-white configuration of a 32 × 32 grid. It makes a finite computational space experientially equivalent to infinity.

TSDNE shares the tension between bounded program and perceived inexhaustibility, but uses seeded synthesis, motion, family variation, and local novelty control rather than exhaustive enumeration.

**Source:** Whitney Artport, *Every Icon*.

#### Casey Reas, *Software Structures* and *Process*

Reas’s work translates instruction-based art into variable time-based systems. It establishes software as both medium and visible behaviour. TSDNE adds one-at-a-time availability, source transfer, local AI, and a non-proprietary politics of circulation.

**Sources:** Casey Reas, *Process*; Processing Foundation overview; Whitney records.

#### Generative screensavers and endless ambient systems

Screensavers, visualisers, and ambient generative systems form a mass-cultural lineage of endlessly changing computational imagery. Their relevance is not merely visual. They normalised the expectation that a computer may continuously produce images without explicit human authorship or completion.

TSDNE differs by making choice, loss, and executable transfer central. It does not operate as background ambience: the visitor’s decision to leave matters.

### 4.5 Browser and net-art genealogies

#### Browser specificity: JODI, Alexei Shulgin, and Form Art

Early net art established that browsers, protocols, interface conventions, errors, windows, links, and HTML controls are artistic material. JODI destabilised expectations of legibility and usability; Shulgin’s *Form Art* repurposed standard controls as graphic matter.

TSDNE is less visibly antagonistic, but its browser materiality is constitutive: WebGL/WebGPU capability, loading, caching, compilation, device limits, local storage, and fallback behaviour all affect the encounter.

#### Olia Lialina, *My Boyfriend Came Back from the War* (1996)

Lialina’s work made browser form, action, and narrative inseparable. Its remix history demonstrates how a work may continue through appropriation. TSDNE changes the consequence from branching narrative to relinquishment of a generated visual process.

**Sources:** Rhizome Net Art Anthology; Rhizome editorial history.

#### Single-serving websites

The single-serving website reduces a domain to one gesture, function, image, or provocation. TSDNE shares this directness but combines it with real-time executable graphics and a deliberately narrow action vocabulary.

The title also belongs to an internet naming tradition in which a domain states the work’s proposition. This makes domain continuity and preservation part of the artwork.

#### Rafaël Rozendaal and websites as collectable public works

Rozendaal’s Art Website Sales Contract distinguishes public access from private collection. TSDNE adopts the distinction between access and relation but rejects a canonical private owner: source export produces a usable copy without exclusion or control over other copies.

**Source:** Art Website Sales Contract.

#### Preservation: Rhizome ArtBase, Net Art Anthology, Conifer, and Webrecorder

Browser works decay through changing APIs, certificates, plug-ins, dependencies, security models, device assumptions, and external services. TSDNE’s dependence on WebGL2, optional WebGPU, model hosts, browser storage, and current JavaScript behaviour makes preservation a design requirement rather than a later archival task.

Preservation should include:

- source and versioned schemas;
- model identifiers and licences;
- representative encounter exports;
- video documentation;
- browser and hardware notes;
- deterministic regeneration tests;
- static fallback documentation;
- archival deposit beyond the live domain.

**Sources:** Rhizome ArtBase; Webrecorder; Net Art Anthology.

### 4.6 Shader cultures, tools, and infrastructures

#### GLSL Sandbox and ShaderToy

ShaderToy is the dominant reference for browser-visible fragment-shader culture. It combines full-screen real-time rendering, source visibility, editable code, persistent URLs, profiles, ratings, comments, and a vast public corpus.

TSDNE inherits ShaderToy’s browser shader ontology while refusing its archive and social interface. The difference is not that TSDNE makes shaders executable—ShaderToy already does—but that execution is embedded in a singular, non-retrievable encounter and source transfer is detached from authorship ranking or platform permanence.

#### The Book of Shaders

*The Book of Shaders* established a widely used pedagogical vocabulary for procedural fragment graphics: coordinate systems, shaping functions, colour, noise, cellular patterns, matrices, and generative composition. It is an enabling source rather than an artistic precedent.

TSDNE’s graph primitives and visual families should remain legible as a authored visual vocabulary, not be disguised as unrestricted machine invention.

#### glslCanvas and glslViewer

Patricio Gonzalez Vivo’s tools support shader execution across webpages and lightweight environments. They demonstrate that shader source can circulate independently of a monolithic application. They are relevant to TSDNE’s export promise and portability tests.

#### twigl and code-golf shader cultures

twigl and related code-golf environments treat minimal source as a creative constraint. This culture intersects strongly with the demoscene and reveals another form of relation between source and image: visual richness is valued in relation to executable brevity.

TSDNE does not optimise for source length, but it should acknowledge that transferable shader source has aesthetic histories independent of AI.

#### Hydra

Hydra is a browser-native live-coding environment for networked video synthesis. It compiles a higher-level language into WebGL operations and supports sharing, collaboration, performance, and improvisation.

Hydra is a major precedent for browser-native moving-image code as a performative object. TSDNE differs by eliminating live manipulation and presenting the generated shader as an encounter rather than an instrument.

#### Shader Park

Shader Park provides a higher-level creative language for constructing shader-based 3D forms. It demonstrates the viability of a constrained domain language rather than raw GLSL generation.

This supports TSDNE’s decision to let a model propose bounded graph or trait mutations rather than unrestricted production shader source.

#### Material Maker and node-graph material systems

Material Maker and similar node-graph tools show how procedural graphics can be represented as editable, typed, compositional graphs. They support reuse, export, and local visual construction.

TSDNE’s typed graph should be understood in this established technical lineage. Its difference is that the graph is generated and usually hidden from the immediate encounter, becoming visible through export rather than editing.

#### ShaderGPT (2026–)

ShaderGPT is a browser-based AI shader authoring service with an editor, saved projects, accounts, and community publication. Its public privacy policy states that it may collect account information, created shader code, usage data, and payment information. It is therefore a useful contemporary counter-model for TSDNE: both concern AI-assisted shader production, but ShaderGPT is organised as a persistent cloud service and creative platform, while TSDNE makes the model optional, local, narrow, and non-essential.

**Sources:** ShaderGPT service and privacy policy.

#### LUMEN (2026)

LUMEN is a browser-native WebGL2 generative shader studio with seeded variation, multiple visual modes, parameter controls, perfect-loop animation, share codes, and PNG, WebM, GIF, and gradient-set export. It is one of the closest implementation neighbours discovered after the initial review.

Its difference is not technical incapacity but interaction form. LUMEN is a studio: it exposes controls, preserves seeds, encourages reproducibility, and presents export as the result of authoring. TSDNE removes the studio interface and turns replacement, non-retrieval, and taking the source into the work’s primary actions.

**Sources:** LUMEN application; `Leonxlnx/lumenshaders` repository.

#### Interactive Shader Format (ISF)

ISF combines GLSL with JSON metadata and a uniform convention. It is a portable interchange format across hosts and an appropriate export target where TSDNE’s generated subset is compatible.

The format also supports the argument that executable source can move among technical and social contexts without requiring exclusive ownership.

#### Shader modules and typed shader ASTs

luma.gl shader modules, ShaderGen, Slang, and thi.ng’s shader AST demonstrate several ways of representing shader code as compositional or typed structures. Their relevance is implementation-level:

- validity can be built structurally;
- modules can carry metadata and requirements;
- source can be generated for multiple targets;
- transformations can be applied before emission;
- cost and compatibility can be analysed.

TSDNE should present its typed graph as a deliberate safety and authorship mechanism, not only a convenience.

### 4.7 Procedural and evolutionary shader generation

#### Quiroz and Dascalu (2016)

Quiroz and Dascalu presented a web application that uses interactive genetic programming to generate vertex shaders for 3D model transformations. The user selects preferred visual effects and the system evolves shader programs.

This is a direct technical precedent for browser-based shader evolution. TSDNE differs in shader type, interaction, and objective: it generates animated fragment images, conceals candidate populations, and makes relinquishment rather than preference selection central.

**Source:** arXiv:1608.05231; SEDE 2016 publication record.

#### Quiroz and Dascalu, *Interactive Shape Perturbation* (2017)

This follow-up to the 2016 procedural vertex-shader web application further specifies the interactive genetic-programming system for perturbing 3D models. It confirms that the earlier work was not an isolated abstract but a continuing browser-oriented implementation lineage.

**Source:** Quiroz and Dascalu, *Interactive Shape Perturbation*, arXiv:1706.04077.

#### Sasso, Loiacono, and Lanzi (2023)

Sasso, Loiacono, and Lanzi describe an interactive evolutionary shader tool using graph representations, mutation, recombination, and feasibility heuristics. The system is integrated into Unity and begins from existing shader structures.

It is a close technical neighbour for typed graph mutation and human evaluation. TSDNE’s difference is again the encounter: no visible population, no explicit optimisation task, no editor workflow, and no requirement that the visitor become a shader designer.

**Source:** arXiv:2312.17587.

#### Procedural material generation research

MatFormer and text/image-conditioned procedural material generation demonstrate machine learning over editable material graphs rather than raster-only outputs. These systems are important because they preserve a structured, reusable representation.

TSDNE’s model is smaller and runs locally, but the wider research supports the decision to generate or mutate graphs rather than only pixels.

**Sources:** Guerrero et al., *MatFormer*, *ACM Transactions on Graphics* 41(4), 2022, DOI 10.1145/3528223.3530173; Hu et al., “Generating Procedural Materials from Text or Image Prompts,” 2023.

### 4.8 LLM and AI shader generation

#### Keijiro Takahashi, AIShader (2023)

AIShader is a proof-of-concept Unity tool that asks ChatGPT to generate shaders. It establishes the immediate feasibility of LLM-to-shader workflows and the familiar repair loop around compilation.

TSDNE differs by avoiding mandatory API use, unrestricted source generation, and editor-centric interaction. Its local model proposes bounded mutations over an authored ontology.

**Source:** AIShader repository.

#### AI Co-Artist (2026)

*AI Co-Artist* uses an LLM-powered framework for interactive GLSL shader animation evolution, explicitly drawing on Picbreeder-like iterative exploration. It appeared in the refereed EvoMUSART 2026 proceedings (LNCS 16523, pp. 192–206, DOI 10.1007/978-3-032-24350-8_13) and is a very close academic neighbour.

Shared features include:

- LLM-supported shader generation;
- iterative visual mutation;
- user involvement in exploration;
- animated GLSL output.

Differences include:

- explicit co-creation and population evolution;
- GPT-4/cloud dependence;
- no irreversible one-object encounter;
- no local privacy or non-property programme.

**Source:** Yuksel and Sawaf, “AI Co-Artist: A LLM-Powered Framework for Interactive GLSL Shader Animation Evolution,” *Artificial Intelligence in Music, Sound, Art and Design*, EvoMUSART 2026, LNCS 16523, pp. 192–206, DOI 10.1007/978-3-032-24350-8_13. The 2025 arXiv version is a preprint of this published chapter.

#### ShadAR (2025)

ShadAR generates shaders in real time from natural-language descriptions for augmented-reality views. It investigates access to visual programming through conversational intent.

TSDNE is not prompt-driven and does not treat language as the main interface. The local model acts behind a narrow visual trait schema and remains subordinate to procedural generation and validation.

**Source:** Mei, Wendt, Mueller, and Gugenheimer, “ShadAR: LLM-driven shader generation to transform visual perception in Augmented Reality,” *2025 IEEE International Symposium on Mixed and Augmented Reality Adjunct*, pp. 959–960, DOI 10.1109/ISMAR-Adjunct68609.2025.00267. The arXiv manuscript was posted in February 2026.

#### Demokritos (2025)

*Demokritos* is the most important internal precedent in Laurenzo’s practice. It concerns interactive, community-centred, self-improving shader generation with LLMs. TSDNE should openly present itself as a conceptual transformation rather than conceal this continuity.

The change is systematic:

| Demokritos | TSDNE |
|---|---|
| community accumulation | singular encounter |
| improvement through shared history | bounded local novelty history |
| interaction directed toward collective system growth | interaction directed toward attachment, relinquishment, and transfer |
| persistent corpus | no public archive |
| social optimisation | refusal of behavioural personalisation |

**Sources:** ISEA 2025 full programme and ISEA Symposium Archives presentation record.

#### Generative adversarial shaders

Research on evolving shader-like programs against neural discriminators shows another relation between machine learning and executable graphics: the neural system evaluates or guides procedural code rather than producing pixels directly.

This is relevant to future TSDNE research but not necessary to the first implementation. It also reinforces the danger of treating a learned evaluator as an objective beauty function.

### 4.9 “This X Does Not Exist”: title, interface, and ideology

#### This Person Does Not Exist (2019)

*This Person Does Not Exist* established the most recognisable version of the title and interface: one synthetic object is shown with almost no surrounding explanation; reloading produces another. It converted model capability into an immediately understandable public demonstration.

TSDNE inherits:

- the title grammar;
- one-output directness;
- serial replacement;
- generative abundance hidden behind a minimal interface.

It changes the object from a static image to executable source and relocates generation from server/pre-generated delivery to local procedural execution plus optional local model mutation.

#### This Waifu Does Not Exist

TWDNE is especially relevant because Gwern documented implementation, pregenerated a very large corpus, attached generated text, discussed compulsive replacement and regret, and adopted public-domain/CC0-oriented circulation.

It is a direct precedent for the poetics of “another.” Its crucial difference is technical and ontological: the visitor receives static outputs from a pregenerated corpus, not a process generated and exportable at encounter time.

#### This Rental Does Not Exist

*This Rental Does Not Exist* combined generated room imagery, host faces, titles, and descriptions into fictional listings. The official account states that the output corpus was pregenerated and the site assembled a new listing every five seconds.

The project foregrounds synthetic plausibility and uncertainty about online representation. TSDNE instead foregrounds executable abstraction and relation.

#### Synthetic-person privacy research

Research showing that “non-existent” faces can resemble real identities or training subjects warns against treating generated output as ontologically detached from source data. TSDNE’s abstract shaders reduce direct biometric risk but do not erase model provenance, training-data, licence, labour, or memorisation concerns.

**Sources:** Webster et al., “This Person (Probably) Exists”; Tinsley, Czajka, and Flynn, “This Face Does Not Exist … But It Might Be Yours!”

## 5. HCI: search, attention, attachment, and reflective interaction

### 5.1 Sequential search and satisficing

TSDNE’s **another** action is a sequential search problem under uncertainty. The visitor observes one option, decides whether it is sufficient, and must abandon it before observing the next. This resembles optimal stopping and satisficing more than choice from a menu.

Relevant variables include:

- perceived distribution of future quality;
- search cost and latency;
- attachment to the current shader;
- confidence that something better exists;
- number of prior replacements;
- whether export is available;
- whether the visitor believes the current object can return.

The interface can therefore be studied without claiming that visitors are optimising rationally.

**Sources:** Caplin, Dean, and Martin, “Search and Satisficing,” *American Economic Review* 101(7), 2011; Simon and Kadane, “Optimal Problem-Solving Search.”

### 5.2 Information foraging

Information foraging models how people trade information value against effort and decide when to leave a source for another. The analogy is productive: each shader is a patch whose potential value can only be assessed by attending to it, while “another” moves to an unknown patch.

The artwork also violates standard efficiency goals. It withholds previews, rankings, recommendations, and back navigation to make attention and loss perceptible.

**Sources:** Pirolli & Card, information foraging; Pirolli, “Rational Analyses of Information Foraging on the Web,” *Cognitive Science* 29(3), 2005.

### 5.3 Choice overload: a necessary correction

TSDNE should not rely on the popular claim that more choice straightforwardly produces paralysis or dissatisfaction. Meta-analytic work finds heterogeneous and sometimes near-zero average effects, strongly dependent on context.

The work’s issue is not merely “too many choices.” It is the temporal and epistemic structure of choice: only one candidate is visible, the set is effectively unenumerable, quality is unknown, and replacement closes the current encounter.

### 5.4 Endowment, possession, and psychological ownership

Research on the endowment effect and possession attachment suggests that possession or felt ownership can alter valuation. Studies also distinguish factual ownership from subjective feelings of ownership, and digital-service research finds forms of attachment to non-material possessions.

TSDNE deliberately separates:

- temporary custody from legal exclusivity;
- source possession from ownership of an aesthetic event;
- encounter attachment from market value;
- taking a copy from depriving others.

A formal study would need to test whether the interface produces subjective ownership; it cannot assume that displaying a shader creates an endowment effect.

**Sources:** Kogut, “Possession Attachment,” *Journal of Behavioral Decision Making* 24(4), 2011; Reb & Connolly, “Possession, Feelings of Ownership and the Endowment Effect”; Fritze et al., digital services and possession attachment.

### 5.5 Reflective, critical, adversarial, and seamful design

TSDNE belongs to HCI traditions that use interfaces to expose rather than erase political and technical conditions:

- **reflective design** treats technology as a site for critical reflection;
- **critical technical practice** joins technical craft to critique of its assumptions;
- **adversarial design** uses designed artefacts to stage political disagreement;
- **seamful design** reveals infrastructural limits rather than performing seamless universality.

The model-status indicator, fallback states, truthful uniqueness language, and export metadata should therefore remain visible enough to resist the fiction of frictionless AI.

**Sources:** Sengers, Boehner, David, and Kaye, “Reflective Design,” *Critical Computing* 2005, DOI 10.1145/1094562.1094569; Philip E. Agre, *Computation and Human Experience*, Cambridge University Press, 1997, DOI 10.1017/CBO9780511571169; Carl DiSalvo, *Adversarial Design*, MIT Press, 2012, DOI 10.7551/mitpress/8732.001.0001; Chalmers, MacColl, and Bell, “Seamful Design: Showing the Seams in Wearable Computing,” IEE Eurowearable 2003, DOI 10.1049/ic:20030140.

### 5.6 Personal without personalisation

Most computational personalisation predicts or shapes an individual through stored behaviour, inferred categories, demographic or contextual signals, and recommender optimisation. TSDNE instead proposes that an encounter becomes personal retrospectively through attention and action.

This is not privacy-preserving personalisation in the usual technical sense. The system does not need to personalise at all. Local recent-history signatures reduce repetition on a device but should not infer identity or taste.

Human–Data Interaction’s principles of legibility, agency, and negotiability remain useful. The visitor should be able to understand what is stored locally, clear it, decline the model, and use the procedural version without penalty.

**Sources:** Mortier et al., “Human–Data Interaction”; local-first software literature; critical recommender-system and privacy scholarship.

### 5.7 The system remembers so that the encounter can forget

TSDNE’s bounded local history has a conceptual function that exceeds novelty suppression. The interface denies retrieval of relinquished shaders, yet the generator retains compact local signatures so that it can avoid returning too quickly to perceptually similar structures. The system therefore remembers in order to make forgetting effective.

This creates three distinct memories:

- **representational memory:** compact graph and visual signatures used for comparison;
- **interface memory:** deliberately absent history, back navigation, and catalogue;
- **human memory:** the visitor’s unstable recollection of what was relinquished.

The asymmetry is central. The machine’s memory is instrumental and bounded; the visitor’s memory is affective and incomplete; the interface performs forgetting. This is not deletion, because exported code and reproducible seeds may persist. It is a designed separation between computational retention and experiential recoverability.

This formulation is stronger than treating IndexedDB as merely an optimisation detail. It connects the non-repetition mechanism directly to the artwork’s poetics of loss, while also requiring transparent controls: the local memory must be inspectable in purpose, bounded in scope, clearable, and never converted into a behavioural profile.

## 6. Political economy: data, property, scarcity, and circulation

### 6.1 Surveillance and data colonialism

Couldry and Mejias describe data colonialism as the appropriation of human life through data relations. TSDNE’s refusal of accounts, remote inference, behavioural profiles, advertising identifiers, and central prompt collection directly responds to this political economy.

The refusal is partial, not absolute:

- the browser and operating system remain platform infrastructures;
- model files are normally downloaded from external hosts;
- CDNs and hosting providers may log requests;
- WebGPU exposes device capabilities and possible fingerprinting surfaces;
- model training has upstream data and labour histories;
- local computation shifts energy and hardware cost to the visitor.

TSDNE should claim reduced project-controlled extraction, not technological exteriority from power.

**Sources:** Couldry and Mejias, “Data Colonialism”; critical data and platform scholarship.

### 6.2 Local-first software

Local-first software advocates keeping primary data and computation under the user’s control while supporting portability and resilience. TSDNE shares several principles:

- generation and inference on the device;
- usable operation without an account;
- local data that can be cleared;
- exportable, non-proprietary artefacts;
- no required project database.

However, TSDNE is not fully local-first in the strongest sense because model and application assets are downloaded, browser storage is platform-dependent, and the work does not synchronise user-owned data across devices.

**Source:** Kleppmann et al., “Local-First Software,” 2019, DOI 10.1145/3359591.3359737.

### 6.3 Free software and source availability

The ability to inspect, run, modify, and redistribute source is more than an export convenience. It changes the spectator’s possible relation to the work.

TSDNE’s source transfer extends Laurenzo’s user-programmer continuum: the visitor may remain a viewer, become a custodian, reuse a shader as a programmer, or redistribute it in another work.

The project must distinguish:

- application licence;
- generated shader licence;
- encounter metadata terms;
- model licence;
- dependency licences;
- rights in training data or generated output that cannot be waived universally.

### 6.4 Generative scarcity and Art Blocks

Art Blocks combines on-chain seeds, deterministic generative scripts, editions, rarity, ownership, and market circulation. It is a central counter-model rather than a merely adjacent platform.

TSDNE preserves seeded computational identity while refusing:

- token allocation;
- fixed editions;
- exclusive ownership;
- rarity as market value;
- a canonical transaction ledger;
- secondary-sale logic.

The contrast should not imply that Art Blocks is homogeneous or reducible to speculation. It shows how technical identity can be converted into proprietary scarcity; TSDNE explores identity without that conversion.

### 6.5 Non-fungibility without NFTs

Every encounter can be non-fungible in the ordinary sense that it is historically and relationally specific without being a non-fungible token. Computational identity, perceptual similarity, and encounter history are different kinds of distinction.

TSDNE should avoid language that accidentally reproduces token logic. An encounter record documents correspondence; it is not a certificate of ownership or uniqueness.

### 6.6 Gift, copy, and transfer

Source export is not a transfer that deprives the origin. It creates a copy and grants permission to continue. This resembles gift, commons, and free-culture circulation more than sale.

The conceptual tension is productive: the visitor “takes” a shader, yet nobody else loses the capacity to generate or use related code. The work separates the feeling of custody from the legal logic of exclusion.

## 7. Browser-local AI and implementation genealogy

### 7.1 WebGL as artistic substrate

WebGL makes GPU fragment and vertex programs available through the browser. It supplies wide deployment, hardware acceleration, and a mature GLSL ES environment, but also introduces:

- implementation variation;
- precision differences;
- driver bugs;
- browser security constraints;
- context loss;
- performance and battery costs;
- accessibility risks from full-screen motion.

TSDNE’s primary renderer should remain WebGL2 because it reaches more devices than mandatory WebGPU and is sufficient for the visual work.

### 7.2 WebGPU and WebLLM

WebLLM demonstrates high-performance in-browser LLM inference through WebGPU and compiled model artefacts. It supports structured generation and local model execution without a required inference server.

Its relevance is direct but should be described accurately:

- model files are downloaded from external storage;
- cold starts are substantial;
- VRAM and memory requirements vary by model and quantisation;
- browser support is uneven;
- inference energy is consumed on the visitor’s device;
- local execution does not erase upstream model provenance.

The model must remain optional. Procedural generation ensures immediate access and preserves the artwork on unsupported devices.

**Sources:** Ruan et al., “WebLLM: A High-Performance In-Browser LLM Inference Engine,” arXiv:2412.15803, 2024; MLC WebLLM repository; W3C WebGPU specification.

### 7.3 Transformers.js and ONNX Runtime Web

Transformers.js and ONNX Runtime Web provide alternative browser inference routes through WebGPU, WebAssembly, or CPU backends. They are relevant to future comparisons and lower-power fallbacks.

They do not remove model conversion, licensing, memory, and compatibility work.

### 7.4 Structured generation and typed intermediate representations

The central implementation decision is to generate or mutate a typed graph rather than accept unrestricted GLSL.

This has artistic and technical consequences:

- the ontology makes authorship explicit;
- invalid type combinations are excluded;
- numerical domains can be constrained;
- cost can be estimated;
- multiple export formats can derive from one source;
- model agency becomes legible and bounded;
- compiler failure cannot dominate the encounter.

The graph should be versioned and included in exports.

### 7.5 Validation, security, and generated code

Generated shaders are untrusted programs even when their primitives are locally authored. Validation must include:

- graph depth and node-count bounds;
- source-size limits;
- no uncontrolled loops;
- numerical-domain protection;
- compile/link failure handling;
- render timeouts and frame-budget checks;
- blank/static/flat/clipped rejection;
- flash and temporal-luminance checks;
- isolated contexts where practical;
- fuzzing and adversarial testing.

DarthShader and related shader-fuzzing research show that GPU shader pipelines are legitimate security surfaces.

### 7.6 Rendering variation and identity

Identical source and seed can render differently across GPUs, drivers, browser precision, colour management, frame timing, and display characteristics. Therefore:

- deterministic graph generation does not guarantee pixel identity;
- pixel difference does not necessarily imply perceptual difference;
- sampled frame similarity can miss motion differences;
- apparent uniqueness cannot be reduced to hashes.

This supports the project’s distinction among computational, perceptual, and encounter identity.

## 8. Accessibility, ecology, and unequal execution

### 8.1 Photosensitive seizure and motion risks

Full-screen generative animation creates risks not solved by interface-level `prefers-reduced-motion` support. The generator itself must control:

- flash frequency;
- temporal contrast;
- large-area luminance change;
- repetitive high-contrast motion;
- motion speed and spatial frequency.

Automated rejection is necessary but not sufficient. External testing and representative manual review are required.

### 8.2 Accessibility beyond motion

A meaningful accessible version should include:

- pause/play;
- reduced-motion and reduced-intensity modes;
- keyboard operation;
- screen-reader labels and announcements;
- non-visual descriptions of the process and current family;
- sufficient interface contrast;
- lower-power fallback;
- truthful explanation of unsupported features.

Accessibility applies across the generated possibility space, not only to curated examples.

### 8.3 Energy and hardware inequality

Local computation relocates rather than eliminates material cost. Visitors with newer GPUs receive model inference and larger candidate searches; lower-power devices receive a reduced path. This creates differentiated access.

The work should disclose:

- model download size;
- memory and GPU requirements;
- data-saver behaviour;
- battery and energy implications;
- fallback differences;
- whether local execution materially reduces or merely shifts server cost.

No general environmental advantage should be claimed without measurement.

### 8.4 Network and model provenance

The procedural baseline can remain small and self-contained. The optional model introduces:

- external hosts;
- mutable URLs;
- licences;
- model-card claims;
- training-data uncertainty;
- cache persistence;
- supply-chain and security dependencies.

Preservation and release records should archive exact model identifiers, checksums where permitted, licences, and expected network requests.

## 9. Perspectives that initially appeared missing

The research identified several perspectives that are easy to omit when the project is framed only as AI shader generation:

1. **Demoscene and executable graphics**, where shader-based runtime generation is already a mature artistic culture.
2. **Sequential search and optimal stopping**, which better describe **another** than generic choice overload.
3. **Information foraging**, because the interface controls when a visitor abandons one patch for another.
4. **Psychological ownership without legal ownership**, relevant to temporary custody and export.
5. **Browser-native and single-serving website histories**, which make the domain and minimal interface part of the work.
6. **Software preservation and re-performance**, necessary for browser and model dependencies.
7. **Local-first software**, which clarifies the precise strengths and limits of local execution.
8. **Data colonialism and platform political economy**, which prevent privacy language from becoming technically naive.
9. **Free-software and gift/copy circulation**, which frame source transfer outside markets.
10. **Generative scarcity and Art Blocks**, the strongest proprietary counter-model.
11. **Shader compiler security and generated-program risk**, including typed fuzzing.
12. **Cross-device rendering divergence**, which complicates uniqueness and reproducibility.
13. **Accessibility over a generated possibility space**, not only interface accessibility.
14. **Energy, device inequality, model provenance, and upstream labour**, which prevent “local AI” from becoming an ethical shortcut.
15. **Digital preservation and re-performance**, necessary for a browser artwork whose dependencies will change.

## 10. Position of TSDNE after the review

### 10.1 What is not new

TSDNE should not claim to originate:

- algorithmic or generative art;
- endless computational image spaces;
- browser-based art;
- single-serving websites;
- the “This X Does Not Exist” title;
- one-output-at-a-time generative demonstration;
- shader sharing or browser shader execution;
- interactive evolutionary art;
- procedural shader evolution;
- LLM-generated shaders;
- browser-local LLM inference;
- seeded computational identity;
- executable or open-source artworks;
- ephemeral digital encounters;
- artistic critique of ownership, surveillance, or personalisation.

### 10.2 What the configuration argues

The research did not locate a verified work with TSDNE’s full configuration, but that fact is secondary. The stronger contribution is an **artistic construction in which each technical and interactional constraint answers a named counter-model and makes the others necessary**:

- immediate procedural generation prevents cloud-model latency from governing first contact;
- optional local AI refuses mandatory central prompt collection while exposing device and provenance limits;
- typed mutation makes the model consequential without making it sovereign;
- one visible shader rejects the archive, feed, and comparison grid as default interfaces to abundance;
- irreversible replacement makes search consequential without manufacturing legal scarcity;
- source export opposes image-only delivery and allows persistence through appropriation rather than platform retention;
- GPL and CC0-oriented release terms permit continuation without exclusivity;
- no behavioural profile allows the encounter to become personal after the fact rather than personalised in advance;
- bounded local memory lets the system remember enough to sustain the visitor’s experience of forgetting;
- accurate uniqueness language refuses both NFT-style artificial scarcity and naive claims that generative difference is self-evident.

The work’s contribution is therefore not vacancy in the literature. It is the necessity and political coherence of the configuration as realised.

### 10.3 Strongest adjacent works requiring explicit discussion in future papers

At minimum, any paper on TSDNE should discuss:

1. *This Person Does Not Exist* and the wider title/interface genre;
2. *This Waifu Does Not Exist* for refresh, attachment, pregeneration, and public-domain circulation;
3. Sims, Picbreeder, Ganbreeder/Artbreeder, LLuminate, and interactive evolutionary computation;
4. *Electric Sheep*;
5. *Every Icon*;
6. AARON;
7. Reas/Processing/software structures;
8. the demoscene, executable graphics, ShaderToy, Hydra, Shader Park, Material Maker, ShaderGPT, LUMEN, and ISF;
9. Quiroz & Dascalu’s 2016 and 2017 systems and Sasso et al.;
10. AIShader, AI Co-Artist, ShadAR, and *Demokritos*;
11. WebLLM and browser-local inference;
12. Art Blocks as a political counter-model;
13. local-first and free-software traditions;
14. HCI work on sequential search, information foraging, and attachment;
15. browser preservation, accessibility, and shader security.
