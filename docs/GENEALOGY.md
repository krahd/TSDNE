# Genealogy and differentiation

## Purpose

This document situates *This Shader Does Not Exist* without attempting a priority claim. Its originality does not reside in inventing browser shaders, seeded procedural generation, novelty search, cryptographic hashing, code export, or anti-repetition. Each component has substantial precedent.

The work’s contribution is the conjunction of these mechanisms into an encounter that refuses profiling, recommendation, certification, title, artificial scarcity, and exclusive possession.

## Procedural and generative art

TSDNE belongs to a long history in which artists author systems rather than fixed images. Relevant lineages include rule-based drawing, algorithmic composition, plotter art, cellular automata, fractals, artificial-life art, demoscene graphics, live coding, and browser-native generative systems.

The shader is already a generative machine. It produces images through execution and time. TSDNE therefore does not require a foundation model to address automation, authorship, variability, or computational abundance.

The generator must be artistically strong, but technical novelty in shader synthesis is not the central claim.

## Browser shader systems

ShaderToy, GLSL Sandbox, The Book of Shaders, numerous creative-coding libraries, and independent projects such as LUMEN establish a mature space of browser-native fragment-shader generation, sharing, looping, and export.

TSDNE should not compete by adding more controls, modes, prompts, or output formats. Its differentiation depends on refusing the catalogue and editor structures that normally organise such systems.

A visitor does not search a shader space. A shader appears.

## Interactive evolutionary and co-creative systems

Interactive evolutionary computation, quality-diversity systems, ShaderGPT, AIShader, *AI Co-Artist*, and the user’s own *Demokritos* demonstrate that model-assisted or evolutionary shader exploration is occupied territory.

TSDNE deliberately withdraws from this trajectory. It does not ask the visitor to select preferred descendants, steer a lineage, describe an intention, or collaborate visibly with an AI agent.

The archived WebLLM branch preserves experiments in that direction, but the public artwork rejects them because they change the category of interaction.

## “Does not exist” and synthetic-media culture

The title enters the cultural field established by projects such as *This Person Does Not Exist* and its many successors. In that field, “does not exist” commonly signifies a plausible synthetic object generated from a learned distribution.

TSDNE reclaims the phrase differently. The shader was not waiting as a catalogue item. It becomes available through execution. The title refers to potentiality and event rather than proof of uniqueness.

The refusal of an LLM or diffusion model is therefore constitutive. The work occupies an AI-coded phrase while demonstrating that generativity, automation, and synthetic abundance do not belong exclusively to foundation models.

## Serendipity and recommender systems

Digital platforms increasingly operationalise discovery through prediction. Recommendation systems infer stable or semi-stable preferences from behaviour and optimise future exposure.

TSDNE separates serendipity from recommendation:

- no profile is built;
- no preference is inferred;
- no attraction vector predicts what should appear next;
- **another** is not treated as a negative rating;
- dwell time is not read as approval;
- every displayed shader enters the same anti-repetition mechanism.

The work’s individualisation is produced by stochastic and procedural difference, not by a model of the visitor.

## Anti-repetition and novelty search

Novelty search and quality-diversity methods provide established techniques for selecting behaviourally different candidates. TSDNE uses a modest maximin novelty tournament as an internal production mechanism.

This is not presented as an algorithmic contribution. Its artistic role is to prevent a shallow generator from falsifying the experience of abundance.

The first encounters receive especially strict separation because early recurrence would collapse the work’s premise before its political and phenomenological structure could become legible.

## Memory and the anti-archive

Software normally uses history to restore, recommend, rank, or accumulate. TSDNE’s local memory does none of these.

It stores lossy perceptual traces that can repel future outputs from already occupied visual regions. It does not preserve the program, seed, graph, or rendered image.

This creates an anti-archive:

> The system remembers enough not to repeat, but not enough to restore.

The mechanism is materially different from a gallery history, browser back button, saved collection, or recommendation profile.

## Cryptographic identity, NFTs, and provenance markets

Cryptographic hashes can identify source material and verify integrity. NFT systems and provenance markets extend such identity into public registries, title claims, scarcity narratives, and transferable value.

TSDNE uses the primitives but refuses that conversion.

> TSDNE retains provenance but abolishes title.

> The shader can be identified, copied, and reconstructed, but not claimed.

The software internally computes hashes. The normal encounter displays none. A technical manifest is available only when the visitor takes the code, and the manifest explicitly states that it is not a certificate.

The work therefore does not deny provenance. It refuses the ceremony that turns provenance into property.

## Anti-copyright and free culture

TSDNE’s gesture is not the transfer of a scarce asset. It is the release of executable material for continued use.

The application is free software under GPL-3.0-or-later. Generated output is offered under a CC0 waiver to the extent waivable rights exist. The wording remains careful because copyright status and third-party rights cannot be resolved by rhetoric alone.

The political proposition is that a relation may matter without exclusion. Copying does not diminish the encounter.

This connects TSDNE to free-software, copyleft, anti-copyright, remix, and media-appropriation traditions, while maintaining the distinction between the authored generator and its generated outputs.

## The user–programmer continuum

Taking the code permits the visitor to move from perception towards technical appropriation. The exported shader may be run, modified, embedded, remixed, or studied.

This does not force everyone to become a programmer. It makes the computational layer available and refuses to seal the artwork inside a proprietary interface.

The mechanism continues concerns developed in Laurenzo’s earlier writing on media appropriation, explicitation, and the user–programmer continuum.

## Ephemerality and software preservation

Software-based art is vulnerable to browser change, dependency failure, hosting loss, and institutional neglect. TSDNE’s public version is deliberately self-contained and static-hostable.

The work distinguishes two forms of ephemerality:

- **interface ephemerality:** a relinquished shader has no route back through the artwork;
- **infrastructural decay:** the artwork itself becomes impossible to execute because dependencies disappear.

TSDNE stages the first while resisting the second. Removing the remote model and checkpoint makes preservation materially stronger.

## Relationship to Demokritos

*Demokritos* investigated community contribution, LLM-generated shaders, and cumulative system improvement. TSDNE is not its smaller successor.

The contrast is productive:

- *Demokritos*: accumulation, community corpus, model intervention, improvement;
- TSDNE: one encounter, no corpus, no learning, no optimisation towards preference, no accumulation visible to the visitor.

TSDNE may be understood as a refusal or inversion of the earlier trajectory.

## Original configuration

No isolated mechanism establishes TSDNE’s originality. The work is defined by the simultaneous presence of:

- one object at a time;
- no prompt;
- no catalogue;
- no comparison grid;
- no back function;
- no profile;
- no recommendation;
- no model training;
- no LLM runtime;
- no ownership;
- no certificate;
- no scarcity claim;
- no public registry;
- no global uniqueness claim;
- no prohibition against copying;
- reconstructive export only through deliberate continuation;
- local memory that preserves difference but not objects.

The resulting proposition is not “a better shader explorer.” It is an encounter with computational possibility organised against the dominant logics of extraction and possession.
