# Genealogy of *This Shader Does Not Exist*

**Reference date:** 4 August 2026  
**Status:** interpretive working genealogy; source readiness is tracked separately  
**Scope:** art history, media theory, browser art, generative systems, shaders, evolutionary computation, artificial intelligence, HCI, political economy, free software, accessibility, security, and preservation

## 1. Method and claim boundary

This genealogy does not attempt to prove that no similar work exists. It performs three narrower tasks:

1. identify precedents for the project’s individual technical and artistic components;
2. identify the strongest historical lineages for its central interaction;
3. define what TSDNE may and may not claim.

The project’s contribution cannot be established by constructing an arbitrarily long conjunction of properties. Conjunctive novelty is cheap: sufficiently specific combinations are almost always unoccupied. The relevant question is whether the constraints form a coherent artistic proposition.

TSDNE’s current proposition is:

> one object, no return, take the source

The work organises computational abundance through interface withdrawal and executable transfer. Its strongest genealogy is therefore not limited to shader generators or local AI.

## 2. Direct interface lineage: “This X Does Not Exist”

### 2.1 *This Person Does Not Exist*

The site established the now-familiar encounter with one synthetic output presented without an author, prompt, archive, or production process in the foreground. Its cultural force came from apparently plausible surface and immediate replacement.

TSDNE inherits:

- one generated object at a time;
- a terse title asserting non-existence;
- immediate browser access;
- refresh as a central gesture.

TSDNE differs because the output is executable and transferable. The title is therefore inverted: the shader demonstrably exists as source and process.

### 2.2 *This Waifu Does Not Exist*

Gwern’s implementation is a particularly close interface precedent. It served pregenerated StyleGAN images and GPT-2 text from a static site, replaced them automatically, allowed immediate refresh and pause, and deliberately omitted rewind or history. Gwern explicitly described history as weakening the experience of loss. The implementation record states that the site contained 100,000 images and 100,000 text snippets and replaced the pair every fifteen seconds.

TSDNE inherits:

- static delivery;
- temporal replacement;
- no rewind;
- the possibility of attachment to an output that may not return.

It differs in encounter-time program generation, executable export, local non-repetition, and explicit rejection of analytics and proprietary scarcity.

Primary record: https://gwern.net/twdne

### 2.3 *This Rental Does Not Exist*

Christopher Schmidt’s project combined generated property imagery and generated listing text. It is relevant as an expansion of the genre from isolated faces to composite, socially legible artefacts.

TSDNE differs by presenting a generative process rather than a plausible representational fiction.

### 2.4 Critical studies of synthetic-face sites

Work such as “This Person (Probably) Exists” and “This Face Does Not Exist … But It Might Be Yours!” complicates the genre’s rhetoric of non-existence by examining training-data resemblance and identity leakage. These studies warn against treating statistical generation as independence from existing people or datasets.

TSDNE does not use a learned image generator for baseline shaders, but the warning remains relevant to any model component and to the title’s ontological claim.

## 3. Generative and algorithmic art

TSDNE belongs to a long history in which artists construct rules, spaces of variation, and relations between determinism and chance.

Relevant precedents include:

- Georg Nees, Frieder Nake, Vera Molnár, and Manfred Mohr;
- Harold Cohen’s AARON;
- Karl Sims’s evolved virtual forms;
- John F. Simon Jr.’s *Every Icon*;
- Casey Reas’s process-based work and Processing;
- browser and software works whose execution, rather than a fixed image, constitutes the artwork.

This lineage establishes that:

- authored rule systems are not new;
- procedural abundance is not new;
- variation within an aesthetic vocabulary is not new;
- code and execution as artistic material are not new.

TSDNE’s question is narrower: what kind of encounter results when procedural abundance is hidden behind one temporary availability and source may be carried away?

## 4. Interactive evolution, exploration, and generated choice

### 4.1 Interactive evolutionary computation

Interactive evolutionary systems place human judgement inside an iterative population. Karl Sims, Hideyuki Takagi, Picbreeder, novelty search, and MAP-Elites establish substantial precedents for exploration through comparison and selection.

### 4.2 Picbreeder, Ganbreeder, and Artbreeder

Picbreeder made collaborative exploration of a generative space a public process. Joel Simon’s Ganbreeder and its descendant Artbreeder brought latent-space combination and attribute manipulation into a browser interface.

TSDNE deliberately refuses several conventions of these systems:

- no population grid;
- no side-by-side comparison;
- no visible fitness process;
- no saved gallery;
- no progressive optimisation toward a declared target.

The refusal is not evidence that TSDNE is more advanced. It establishes a different temporal and affective structure: judgement without comparison and replacement without return.

### 4.3 LLuminate

Joel Simon’s LLuminate is relevant to browser-based model interaction and exploratory visual generation. It reinforces that local or browser-facing generative interfaces are an occupied field.

## 5. Shader cultures and executable graphics

### 5.1 ShaderToy and shader-sharing platforms

ShaderToy made fragment shaders publicly executable, inspectable, forkable, discussable, and archived. It is the clearest counter-model for TSDNE’s refusal of a searchable public catalogue.

TSDNE adopts:

- executable fragment programs;
- browser rendering;
- portable source;
- adaptation to ShaderToy’s conventions.

It refuses:

- canonical public accumulation;
- ranking and social visibility;
- retrieval through a permanent gallery.

### 5.2 Educational and live-coding systems

*The Book of Shaders*, glslCanvas, glslViewer, twigl, Hydra, Shader Park, ISF, and Material Maker demonstrate multiple ways to teach, perform, compose, exchange, and embed shader logic.

These systems establish that source portability and browser execution are technical conventions, not TSDNE inventions.

### 5.3 Demoscene 4k and 64k intros

The demoscene is a necessary historical lineage. Size-constrained intros treat executable graphics and sound as complete works, circulate source or binaries, and value runtime generation under severe limits. They show that an executable visual process can be an artwork rather than merely a tool or screen effect.

TSDNE differs in delivery, source waiver, visitor withdrawal, and absence of competition metrics, but it should not narrate portable shader execution without this history.

### 5.4 Screensavers and distributed generative displays

Electric Sheep and related systems establish endless networked visual generation, mutation, distributed rendering, and user-influenced selection. TSDNE differs by rejecting population display, explicit voting, and public accumulation.

## 6. Procedural and evolutionary shader generation

### 6.1 Quiroz and Dascalu

“Design and Implementation of a Procedural Content Generation Web Application for Vertex Shaders” (2016) and “Interactive Shape Perturbation” (2017) describe browser applications using interactive genetic programming to evolve vertex-shader programs.

These are direct technical precedents for:

- browser shader generation;
- program representations;
- interactive evolutionary search;
- visual selection of shader effects.

TSDNE cannot claim invention of procedurally generated shaders or browser-based shader evolution.

### 6.2 Sasso, Loiacono, and Lanzi

“A Tool for the Procedural Generation of Shaders using Interactive Evolutionary Algorithms” (2023) is another direct precedent for shader generation through interactive evolutionary methods.

### 6.3 Learned procedural materials

MatFormer and work on generating procedural materials from text or image prompts demonstrate learned generation of structured material graphs. They are relevant to the future typed-program direction but exceed the current TSDNE alpha, which uses authored family templates and scalar traits.

## 7. Language models and shaders

### 7.1 AIShader

Keijiro Takahashi’s AIShader demonstrates prompt-driven shader generation through a language model. It establishes that “LLM writes shader code” is not a TSDNE contribution.

### 7.2 Demokritos

Santiago Ferrando, Christian Clark, and Tomas Laurenzo’s *Demokritos: Interactive, Community-Centred, Self-Improving Shader Generation using Large Language Models* was presented in the ISEA 2025 poster programme and appears in the symposium record.

Demokritos is the user’s direct prior work. It centres community accumulation and iterative model-supported shader generation. TSDNE inverts that structure:

- singular encounter rather than community corpus;
- withdrawal rather than accumulation;
- no public improvement history;
- executable taking without ownership.

Author order must remain Ferrando, Clark, Laurenzo.

Programme record: https://isea2025.isea-international.org/program/full-program/

### 7.3 AI Co-Artist

Kamer Ali Yuksel and Hassan Sawaf’s “AI Co-Artist: A LLM-Powered Framework for Interactive GLSL Shader Animation Evolution” was published in the EvoMUSART 2026 proceedings, LNCS 16523, pp. 192–206.

It is a direct precedent for language-model-supported iterative shader evolution. TSDNE’s differences lie in one-object encounter, absence of visible populations, opt-in local execution, deterministic validation, and withdrawal.

### 7.4 ShadAR

Three records must be distinguished:

1. the four-author IEEE ISMAR-Adjunct 2025 publication, pp. 959–960, DOI `10.1109/ISMAR-Adjunct68609.2025.00267`;
2. arXiv:2602.17481, posted in February 2026, presenting the author manuscript associated with that work;
3. the expanded five-author CHI EA 2026 publication, DOI `10.1145/3772363.3799378`, which adds object detection to support selective manipulation.

ShadAR establishes LLM-generated shaders as an AR prototyping mechanism rather than a novelty unique to TSDNE.

### 7.5 The two ShaderGPT projects

Two projects sharing the name must not be conflated.

- `shadergpt.14islands.com` is a 14islands experiment announced in February 2025 for comparing model-generated GLSL.
- `shadergpt.com` is a separate account-based service with its own operator and privacy policy.

The second may function as a cloud-service counter-model, but its policies must not be attributed to the first.

### 7.6 LUMEN

LUMEN, maintained by the GitHub account `Leonxlnx` (display name “Leon Lin”), is a zero-dependency WebGL2 generative shader studio with multiple modes, seeds, share codes, loops, and media export.

It is a close technical neighbour. It reinforces that TSDNE should not claim technical novelty for a static browser shader studio. Its major difference is interaction structure: LUMEN is an authoring and retrieval environment; TSDNE restricts the visitor to one temporary encounter and executable taking.

## 8. Browser-local inference

WebLLM, Transformers.js, ONNX Runtime Web, WebGPU, and WebAssembly establish browser-local model execution as a substantial technical field.

TSDNE adopts optional WebLLM inference but must preserve several distinctions:

- local inference is not offline operation;
- model and runtime files may be remotely requested;
- external hosts receive ordinary metadata;
- browser execution does not resolve model provenance;
- visitor hardware bears memory and energy costs;
- device inequality affects access.

The alpha therefore makes local AI opt-in and off by default.

## 9. Taking, depletion, and endless supply

### 9.1 Felix Gonzalez-Torres

Gonzalez-Torres’s candy works and paper stacks are among the closest art-historical precedents for TSDNE’s **use this shader** gesture.

MoMA describes the candy works as unique works in which visitors may take individual pieces and carry them away. As pieces are taken, the displayed form changes; exhibitors decide whether and when to replenish it. The paper stacks similarly combine unique work status with “endless supply.”

Relevant relations:

- a visitor takes part of what is displayed;
- the taken element circulates outside the exhibition;
- taking does not necessarily destroy the work’s identity;
- supply can be conceptually endless while presentation changes;
- custody, ownership, and carried material are not identical.

Critical difference:

TSDNE must not appropriate the bodily, memorial, political, and AIDS-related meanings of works such as *“Untitled” (Portrait of Ross in L.A.)*. The relation is structural, not an equivalence of content.

Institutional source: https://www.moma.org/collection/artists/2233

### 9.2 Gift and circulation

Mauss and Lewis Hyde provide relevant vocabularies for gifts, obligation, circulation, and the persistence of relation through transfer. They should be used cautiously: a downloadable shader is not automatically a gift in the anthropological sense, and frictionless copying may lack the reciprocal structure central to gift theory.

## 10. Refusal of documentation and encounter-dependent work

### 10.1 Tino Sehgal

Sehgal’s constructed situations are major precedents for encounter-dependent work and refusal of conventional documentation. Institutional records describe works that prohibit photographs, video, and material documentation and exist through enacted situations and oral transmission.

TSDNE differs fundamentally:

- it necessarily produces executable code;
- browsers and storage leave traces;
- export is encouraged rather than prohibited;
- interface non-retrieval cannot eliminate forensic persistence.

Sehgal nevertheless clarifies that non-documentation can be constitutive rather than merely an absent feature.

Institutional sources:

- https://hirshhorn.si.edu/exhibitions/tino-sehgal-this-you/
- https://www.walkerart.org/collections/artist/tino-sehgal/

### 10.2 Browser and net art

JODI, Olia Lialina, Rafaël Rozendaal, Rhizome’s ArtBase and Net Art Anthology, and Webrecorder establish the browser as artistic site and expose tensions among liveness, ownership, migration, decay, emulation, and preservation.

Rozendaal’s website-sales contract is relevant to the relationship among work, domain, public accessibility, and ownership. TSDNE differs by rejecting exclusive title in generated shader outputs.

## 11. Digital memory, storage, and materiality

### 11.1 Wendy Hui Kyong Chun

“The Enduring Ephemeral, or the Future Is a Memory” (*Critical Inquiry* 35.1, 2008, pp. 148–171) is non-negotiable for TSDNE.

The essay examines the relation among memory, storage, endurance, and regeneration in digital media. TSDNE’s local trace and deterministic regeneration must be read within this existing argument.

“The system remembers so that the encounter can forget” may remain as a description of the work’s configuration, but not as a claim of theoretical priority.

Publisher record: https://doi.org/10.1086/595632

### 11.2 Matthew G. Kirschenbaum

*Mechanisms: New Media and the Forensic Imagination* distinguishes formal and forensic materiality and emphasises storage, erasure, variability, repeatability, and survivability.

This distinction is directly useful:

- formally, the interface offers no route back;
- forensically, seeds, caches, program records, browser storage, files, and devices may persist.

The artwork’s strongest problem lies in the mismatch between those levels.

Publisher record: https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262517409/mechanisms/

### 11.3 Preservation

Rhizome ArtBase and Webrecorder demonstrate that browser artworks require deliberate preservation of code, dependencies, network conditions, documentation, and performance context.

TSDNE’s refusal of visitor retrieval does not excuse the artist from preserving the work. Preservation should include:

- source and release history;
- generator schema;
- model and runtime versions;
- representative encounters;
- screenshots and video;
- browser and hardware notes;
- dependency licences;
- re-performance instructions.

## 12. Search, stopping, attachment, and judgement

HCI and behavioural research on satisficing, information foraging, possession attachment, and endowment effects supplies hypotheses for the encounter.

It does not establish TSDNE’s effects in advance.

The following remain empirical questions:

- whether no-return replacement changes stopping;
- whether temporary availability produces attachment;
- whether export changes perceived responsibility;
- whether disclosure of reproducibility weakens or intensifies loss;
- whether visitors distinguish personal meaning from personalisation.

Dwell time, replacement, and export must not be treated as direct measures of preference without validation.

## 13. Personalisation, identity, and recommender systems

John Cheney-Lippold’s *We Are Data*, Nick Seaver’s *Computing Taste*, Human–Data Interaction, and work on recommender systems provide a more appropriate context for discussing personalisation than an abstract opposition between “personal” and “profiled.”

TSDNE does not currently individualise output to a person. It provides randomised and locally de-duplicated delivery. A visitor may form a personal relation after the encounter, but that is not itself a new alternative personalisation technology.

The phrase “personal without personalisation” should therefore remain a question requiring evidence, not the principal established contribution.

## 14. Relational aesthetics and critique

If TSDNE uses the language of relation rather than property, it enters debates around relational aesthetics. Nicolas Bourriaud should not be cited without the substantial critiques, especially Claire Bishop’s argument that convivial or participatory relations do not become politically meaningful merely by occurring.

This matters because many of TSDNE’s refusals are invisible unless disclosed. The work must make its technical and political seams inspectable rather than relying on paratext alone.

## 15. Free software, ownership, and artificial scarcity

### 15.1 Free software

The GPL on the authored generator is the project’s strongest material intervention in circulation. It governs code that is clearly authored and supports inspection, modification, and redistribution.

Christopher Kelty’s account of recursive publics may be useful for connecting executable circulation with the communities and infrastructures required to maintain it.

### 15.2 CC0 outputs

CC0 clarifies the intended circulation of generated outputs where waivable rights exist. It should not be dramatised as surrender of a property right that necessarily attached to every output.

### 15.3 Art Blocks and NFTs

Art Blocks and related generative-token systems provide a counter-model in which algorithmic variation is tied to token allocation, editioning, provenance, market exchange, and scarcity.

TSDNE refuses:

- token ownership;
- edition scarcity;
- canonical buyer records;
- market allocation;
- exclusive control over copies.

It still creates **interface scarcity**: only one encounter is available at a time. This is temporal and experiential organisation, not low computational supply.

## 16. Security, visual safety, and unequal access

### 16.1 Shader security

WebGL and WebGPU pass generated programs through complex browser and GPU stacks. DarthShader demonstrates that shader translators and compilers remain a meaningful attack surface.

TSDNE mitigates risk by generating from authored templates rather than executing unrestricted model-written GLSL. This does not eliminate browser, driver, dependency, or denial-of-service risks.

### 16.2 Accessibility

WCAG, reduced-motion practices, and photosensitive-content guidance establish that animation must be controllable and high-frequency luminance change cannot be evaluated from sparse frames.

The alpha now separates broad motion descriptors from a dense screen-level luminance heuristic. External review remains mandatory.

### 16.3 Device inequality

Optional local inference excludes or burdens devices differently. Local computation may shift cost from a central service to the visitor. The work should expose this condition and remain complete without the model.

## 17. Necessity rather than vacancy

The defensible contribution is not “nobody combined these features.” Each constraint answers a specific counter-model:

| TSDNE constraint | Counter-model or problem | Function in the work |
|---|---|---|
| One object at a time | population grids, catalogues, authoring studios | judgement without simultaneous comparison |
| No interface return | archives and saved histories | replacement becomes withdrawal rather than browsing |
| Executable export | image-only generative sites | the process may persist outside the encounter |
| No exclusive title | tokenised generative scarcity | circulation without ownership certificate |
| Bounded local trace | immediate repetition | system memory supports non-retrieval without a central registry |
| No preference model | recommender personalisation | no advance fitting to a visitor |
| Optional local model | mandatory cloud inference | model participation without central prompt collection, but with disclosed external assets and device costs |
| Transparent provenance | AI mystification | authored, random, procedural, and model actions remain distinguishable |

The constraints are meaningful only insofar as they work together in the realised encounter.

## 19. Current claim register

Permitted:

> TSDNE presents one generated shader at a time, prevents retrieval of replaced encounters through its interface, and permits the current executable source to circulate without exclusivity.

Permitted:

> The current alpha uses authored shader families, local candidate selection, bounded encounter traces, and an optional validated local-model proposal mechanism.

Not permitted:

- first AI shader artwork;
- first browser shader generator;
- absolute visual uniqueness;
- universal disappearance;
- clinically safe animation;
- personalisation without profiling as an established result;
- meaningful or superior model agency without ablation;
- original theory of digital memory;
- proof that no similar work exists.

## 20. Work still required

- human reading of every source used in a manuscript;
- visual review of at least 200 encounters;
- controlled comparison of model, random, and authored mutations;
- public or exhibition encounter documentation;
- external accessibility and security review;
- final authorial rewriting;
- continued search for works organised around taking, withdrawal, executable transfer, and non-retrieval.

This genealogy is a research instrument. It is not evidence that those tasks are complete.
