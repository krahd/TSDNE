# Project specification

## Project statement

*This Shader Does Not Exist* is a browser artwork that gives each visitor one full-screen, aesthetically resolved, continuously animated shader. The visitor may remain with it, request another in the hope of receiving one they prefer, or choose **use this shader** and receive the exact source code and concise instructions for inserting it into a webpage or program.

The shader is ephemeral as an encounter. Once relinquished, it is no longer recoverable through the interface. The site offers no catalogue and no history. A shader may persist only if a visitor takes it into another technical and social context.

The artwork resides in the relation among desire, chance, taste, abundance, scarcity, executable code, and disappearance. Refreshing is a wager: the visitor must abandon the current image without knowing whether the next will be preferable.

## Core experience

- The page opens directly into a full-screen shader.
- The interface is minimal and initially unobtrusive.
- **another** relinquishes the current shader and produces a new encounter.
- **use this shader** reveals verified source and integration templates.
- **about** explains the work without interrupting the visual field.
- There is no public archive, ranking, marketplace, or gallery.
- Visual quality is a constitutive artistic requirement.
- The system attempts to minimise perceptual repetition, while making no absolute claim that similar images can never recur.

## Ephemerality and transfer

TSDNE distinguishes two forms of persistence.

### Ephemeral availability

The site does not permit visitors to return to a previous shader. The encounter ends when the page is closed or another shader is requested.

### Persistent appropriation

A visitor may take the shader while it is present. The shader then disappears from TSDNE but may continue as code in another webpage, program, artwork, archive, or derivative work. The work therefore stages a transfer rather than unverifiable deletion.

## Aesthetic field

The generator should support a broad but recognisable field of real-time abstract images. Initial families may include:

- fluid and turbulent fields;
- interference and moiré structures;
- cellular and Voronoi systems;
- radial, polar, and tunnel transformations;
- signed-distance-field compositions;
- layered waves, folds, and coordinate warps;
- restrained uses of symmetry, repetition, grain, and feedback.

Variation must occur in structure, not merely palette or numeric parameters. The system should support differences in spatial organisation, rhythm, scale, depth, texture, density, colour, symmetry, and temporal behaviour.

## What the work is not

TSDNE is not primarily:

- an AI demonstration;
- a prompt interface;
- a shader marketplace;
- an archive of generated code;
- a code editor;
- a universal definition of beauty;
- a claim to have invented generative or ephemeral shader art.

## Relationship to Laurenzo's practice

The project continues a practice in which interaction, computation, and interfaces construct political and aesthetic situations rather than functioning as neutral tools. It extends the user-programmer continuum and media appropriation by allowing a spectator to become the temporary custodian and potential re-user of executable visual code. The work also continues the shader research developed in *Demokritos*, but replaces community accumulation and iterative improvement with singular encounter, relinquishment, and transfer.

## Research questions

- How does irreversible refresh affect attention, judgement, attachment, and regret?
- How do visitors decide that a shader is good enough to stop searching?
- What happens when an ephemeral artwork can be taken away as executable code?
- How do abundance and scarcity coexist in generative systems?
- What is the relation between ownership of source code and ownership of an aesthetic event?
- Can a system reduce perceptual repetition without reducing visual diversity to one optimisation target?
- How do structural, pixel-level, perceptual, and temporal similarity measures disagree?
- Does the export path transform spectators into users, collectors, programmers, or distributors?
- What new histories emerge when claimed shaders appear in other sites and works?

## Possible outputs

### Artwork

- public browser work;
- gallery installation with one or several simultaneous encounters;
- projected public version;
- printed or video documentation of relinquished encounters;
- an atlas of places where claimed shaders later appear, submitted voluntarily by users.

### Papers

- practice-based paper on ephemerality, refresh, desire, and executable transfer;
- aesthetic-computing paper on visual quality and non-repetition;
- HCI paper on attention, stopping behaviour, attachment, and regret;
- technical paper on browser-native constrained program synthesis and dynamic-output comparison;
- software-studies paper on code as image, gift, commodity, and portable technical object.

## Constraints

- no paid inference or API;
- no required server computation;
- no required database;
- static deployment must remain possible;
- mobile and lower-power devices must receive a meaningful version;
- exported code must be tested and truthful;
- no hidden collection of identifying or behavioural data by default.
