# Complete revised TSDNE goal

## Goal

Produce a complete, public-release candidate of *This Shader Does Not Exist* in which the artwork, interface, software architecture, documentation, and first paper all enact the same proposition:

> A singular computational encounter can be personal without behavioural personalisation, identifiable without title, reproducible without collectibility, and freely circulating without manufactured scarcity.

The public artwork must not be a shader editor, prompt interface, recommender, evolutionary design tool, or model demonstration.

## Frozen conceptual decisions

1. The encounter is the work’s primary material.
2. One shader appears at a time.
3. **another** relinquishes the present shader through the interface; it is not interpreted as dislike or preference.
4. **take the code** permits the shader to continue elsewhere under a non-exclusive free-use position.
5. No catalogue, gallery, restore function, public archive, account, profile, recommendation process, or ownership registry is provided.
6. No language model is used in the public artwork.
7. The title enters the cultural space of AI-generated “does not exist” projects while refusing the foundation-model apparatus usually associated with that phrase.
8. Internal hashes and manifests remain technical instruments. No certificate is issued.
9. **TSDNE retains provenance but abolishes title.**
10. **The shader can be identified, copied, and reconstructed, but not claimed.**
11. A bounded local anti-repetition memory stores only non-reconstructive perceptual traces.
12. Shader richness is achieved through authored procedural grammars, computational search, perceptual validation, and curation rather than a language model.

## Software acceptance criteria

The revised version is complete when:

- a valid shader appears without a model download or external runtime dependency;
- the generator produces visually complex animated programs across multiple macro-families;
- the first encounters enforce strong family and perceptual separation;
- repeat visits use local perceptual traces to avoid obvious recurrence;
- the stored trace cannot reconstruct a seed, graph, source program, or image;
- candidate generation uses local novelty-and-quality search without modelling the visitor;
- **another** never changes future outputs because of inferred taste;
- **take the code** exports reconstructable source and a technical manifest;
- exported technical data explicitly confers no title, ownership, exclusivity, authenticity, scarcity, or uniqueness;
- no hash, serial, score, rarity, or provenance token is shown in the normal encounter;
- privacy is true by architecture rather than policy alone;
- reduced-motion, reduced-intensity, pause, keyboard operation, and flash screening remain supported;
- all source is static-hostable and preservation does not depend on a model host;
- automated tests cover provenance, anti-repetition traces, novelty selection, exports, and deterministic generation.

## Writing acceptance criteria

The repository and paper must consistently distinguish:

- singular encounter from unique object;
- individualisation from personalisation;
- provenance from title;
- technical identity from ownership;
- interface loss from global destruction;
- free circulation from acquisition;
- local anti-repetition memory from behavioural profiling;
- generative computation from foundation-model AI.

No public-facing text may describe the shader as collectible, owned, minted, claimed, certified, rare, guaranteed unique, or permanently destroyed.
