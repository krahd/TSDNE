# AI and computational-method statement

## Public artwork

The public version of *This Shader Does Not Exist* does not use a language model, diffusion model, remote inference service, third-party generative API, or learned generative checkpoint at runtime.

Its visual output is produced by explicit procedural programs executed locally in the visitor’s browser. Candidate selection uses deterministic and stochastic computational techniques, perceptual measurement, technical validation, and novelty-oriented search.

## Why this matters

The absence of a foundation model at runtime is not a claim that procedural systems are immaterial, neutral, or free of infrastructure. The artwork still depends on browsers, hardware, electricity, hosting, standards, prior mathematical and software traditions, and an explicit visual genealogy.

The narrower runtime claim is that TSDNE does not require:

- a scraped training corpus at execution time;
- an opaque generative checkpoint;
- remote model hosting;
- inference logging;
- a high-memory model runtime;
- unresolved runtime model-output provenance;
- behavioural data from the visitor.

This makes the work’s privacy, preservation, licensing, and device-access position substantially clearer.

## Relation to artificial intelligence

TSDNE nevertheless enters the cultural field of artificial intelligence. Its title invokes the contemporary genre of synthetic entities that “do not exist,” and the work addresses conditions widely associated with generative AI:

- inexhaustible plausible output;
- automated production;
- uncertain authorship;
- abundance without prior inventory;
- the instability of originality and ownership;
- the difference between computational and perceptual identity.

The project contests the equation of generativity with foundation models. Its runtime refusal is part of the artwork.

## Non-LLM computational search

The system generates several candidate programs and evaluates them locally. It uses:

- explicit generative grammars;
- deterministic pseudo-randomness from cryptographic seeds;
- WebGL2 rendering;
- perceptual descriptors;
- quality and safety constraints;
- maximin novelty selection;
- bounded local anti-repetition memory.

These methods do not infer the visitor’s preferences and do not construct a behavioural model.

## Development assistance

The runtime position must be distinguished from the development process.

Project documentation and software development use contemporary programming assistants. The 7 August 2026 expansion of the procedural grammar from twelve to thirty-nine families was committed with Claude Opus 5 explicitly credited as a co-author of that code revision. Other project development has also involved AI-assisted programming, review, and drafting under Tomas Laurenzo’s direction.

This assistance does not run in the public artwork and does not receive visitor data. It does, however, form part of the software’s production history and should be disclosed in scholarly writing where authorship, labour, or the refusal of foundation-model runtime is discussed.

Accordingly, **authored procedural system** in project documents does not mean “written without AI assistance.” It means that the operative generative rules are explicit, inspectable, editable, licensed within the project, and accepted through an accountable development and curation process rather than generated at visitor runtime from an opaque learned corpus.

All committed code, concepts, claims, references, licences, and artistic decisions remain subject to the artist’s review and responsibility.

## Explicit visual genealogy

The generator is not culturally source-free. Its current visual vocabulary includes explicit references to histories of abstract cinema, geometric abstraction, print/reprographic practice, mathematics, computer graphics, biological form, and physical systems.

Named families such as `richter-rhythm`, `eggeling-diagonal`, `suprematist-drift`, and `bauhaus-tiles` should therefore be understood as declared formal references, not as evidence of a supposedly autonomous or originless procedural aesthetic.

This explicit genealogy is materially different from an opaque learned corpus, but it does not eliminate questions of influence, appropriation, authorship, or labour. Those questions belong in the artwork’s documentation and paper rather than being erased by the absence of a runtime model.

## Future non-LLM methods

The following may be explored only when they improve the visual work and preserve the encounter:

- reaction–diffusion;
- cellular automata;
- neural cellular automata trained on wholly controlled material;
- compositional pattern-producing networks;
- small authored neural dynamical systems;
- evolutionary and quality-diversity search;
- coupled oscillators and reservoir systems.

Their inclusion would not be advertised as novelty or as a generic claim of “AI.” Each method would require a separate audit of training material, reproducibility, compute, licensing, preservation, and device access.
