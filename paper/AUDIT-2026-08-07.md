# Audit of the first TSDNE journal manuscript

**Manuscript:** `paper/MANUSCRIPT.md`  
**Audit date:** 7 August 2026  
**Purpose:** adversarial claim, source, register, and implementation audit before second draft

## Overall judgement

The first manuscript has the correct paper inside it. Its central relocation argument is materially supported by the artwork and much stronger than the former “personal without personalisation” thesis. The paper should remain one substantial journal article.

It is not ready to freeze. The principal weaknesses are no longer conceptual confusion about the project but four forms of overreach or displacement:

1. the opening begins from a generic diagnosis of computational abundance rather than from the apparatus;
2. the art-historical field is still weaker than the technical/platform field at exactly the point where taking, circulation, depletion, and non-exclusive continuation become central;
3. a few formulations are stronger than the implementation can establish;
4. several passages retain the balanced, enumerative rhythm of an external scholarly commentator rather than Laurenzo's sharper apparatus–contradiction register.

## 1. Opening: revise

Current opening:

> Computational abundance is usually presented as an advantage. A catalogue grows. A feed never ends.

This is competent but generic. It could introduce many papers on generative media. It also violates the strongest compositional pattern in the source writing: apparatus first, abstraction earned through the contradiction generated by the apparatus.

### Revision

Begin with the event:

> A shader appears before the visitor has asked for one.

Describe the visible work, **another**, and **take the code**. Only then identify the strange relation to computational abundance. This puts the reader inside the contradiction before naming it.

## 2. González-Torres: add as an exact counterpoint

The manuscript currently moves from digital scarcity into crypto art and tokens without a sufficiently strong art-historical precedent for taking and endless reproducibility.

Felix Gonzalez-Torres is not a decorative related-work addition. His paper stacks and candy works are materially relevant because:

- visitors are invited to take part of the displayed work;
- paper stacks can be conceptualised as an endless supply;
- candy works diminish as viewers take from them and may be replenished;
- distribution and circulation are constitutive rather than accidental;
- the work can remain singular as a work while its material disperses.

MoMA's current artist account explicitly describes paper stacks as unique works in “endless supply”, viewers carrying pieces away, candy works changing as viewers take from them, and the relation of letting go and giving back to the public. MoMA's record for *Untitled (Death by Gun)* describes an “endless” edition whose sheets are taken away and reprinted as the stack is depleted. These are directly relevant countermodels.

### Important difference

Do not claim equivalence. In Gonzalez-Torres, taking can materially diminish the displayed manifestation and replenishment is part of the work's variable realisation. In TSDNE, **take the code** duplicates a computational object without depleting the shader still running in the browser. Depletion occurs elsewhere: **another** withdraws interface access to the current encounter.

This difference strengthens the paper's main claim: TSDNE separates taking from depletion and relocates depletion into access.

### Working sources

- C. Ondine Chavoya, MoMA artist text on Felix Gonzalez-Torres, 2025; authoritative museum account, with primary interview references to Gonzalez-Torres and Tim Rollins (1993).
- Deborah Wye, *Artists and Prints: Masterworks from The Museum of Modern Art*, 2004, p. 245, reproduced on MoMA's record for *Untitled (Death by Gun)*.
- MoMA collection record for *Untitled (Placebo)*, 1991.

Final manuscript should preferably cite the relevant Gonzalez-Torres interview and/or a stable scholarly catalogue in addition to museum metadata once read.

## 3. Benjamin: remove for now

The current scarcity section opens through Benjamin. The reference is not wrong, but it currently performs generic historical authority rather than a task that Chun, Kirschenbaum, Gonzalez-Torres, the author's prior artificial-scarcity argument, and the crypto-art countermodel cannot perform more precisely.

The source ledger also marks the edition/page verification as unresolved.

### Revision

Remove Benjamin from the second draft unless a later close reading establishes a specific function that cannot be replaced by more precise sources. This makes the bibliography more selective and avoids a canonical citation functioning as atmosphere.

## 4. Title genealogy: source more precisely

Current manuscript says:

> “This X Does Not Exist” sites became shorthand for generative models...

This is broadly correct but uncited.

### Verified source position

*This Person Does Not Exist* was created by Philip Wang in 2019 using NVIDIA StyleGAN code; contemporary reporting describes the site producing a new synthetic face on refresh. The original domain is currently for sale, so it should not be treated as a stable contemporary primary source.

Use either:

- Matthew Gault's February 2019 Motherboard/VICE report for the website's historical operation and creator;
- Karras, Laine, and Aila's StyleGAN paper for the underlying generative architecture;
- later scholarly discussions only where a peer-reviewed source is actually needed.

The title discussion does not require a long genealogy. One precise historical citation is enough.

## 5. Privacy claim: narrow

Current manuscript:

> The visitor cannot be reconstructed from it as a taste profile, and the shader cannot be reconstructed from it as an object.

The second clause is defensible from the stored schema: the persistent trace contains `localId`, `createdAt`, `family`, and compact perceptual descriptors, but no seed, graph, source, or frame.

The first clause is too absolute. The trace is deliberately not a taste representation and contains no explicit identity or preference field, but “cannot be reconstructed” makes a stronger security/information-theoretic claim than has been demonstrated.

### Revision

Use:

> The stored trace is neither an identity record nor a preference representation, and TSDNE does not interpret it as either. It is also intentionally insufficient to reconstruct the shader that produced it.

This preserves the politics without making an untested impossibility claim.

## 6. Public family count: correct, but source contains dormant builders

The manuscript states that the public generator contains thirty-nine structural families. This is correct because the public `FAMILY_NAMES` list contains thirty-nine entries and all public sampling proceeds through that list.

However, current `generator.js` also contains complete builders and metadata for eleven additional names that are not present in `FAMILY_NAMES`:

- `wireframe-solid`;
- `stereo-parallax`;
- `grid-horizon`;
- `memphis-pattern`;
- `latent-wash`;
- `volume-tomogram`;
- `caustic-pool`;
- `newton-basins`;
- `rule-automaton`;
- `grammar-spiral`;
- `branch-growth`.

These are dormant/experimental technical possibilities, not public families. Do not silently count them as part of the artwork and do not promote them merely because code exists. They should be evaluated through private curation before inclusion or removed/quarantined later.

The manuscript does not need this technical residue unless it becomes part of the production history.

## 7. Candidate sampling: previous audit issue resolved

The August 7 audit correctly identified that the previous `familySchedule()` walked a categorically ordered list from a random offset and could stop after sampling a category neighbourhood.

This has now been corrected on `main`:

- public families are grouped by broad macro-category;
- families are shuffled within categories;
- candidate attempt order is interleaved across categories before returning to a category;
- tests establish coverage of all public families and macro-category crossing in the first cycle.

The manuscript may therefore describe the current selector as sampling across broad visual categories. It should not describe the public selector as a statistically balanced design; that role belongs only to the private generator audit.

## 8. Memory clear: previous audit issue resolved

The public **clear local anti-repetition memory** operation now clears:

- persistent local traces;
- in-memory recent trace list;
- in-memory recent-family list;
- session-only recent colour-mode list.

The manuscript's statement that the clear operation resets the corresponding anti-repetition state is therefore aligned with current `main`.

## 9. Private curation: distinguish implemented instrument from absent results

The revised branch `research/curation-instrument-250-v2` now exists and implements the balanced generator-audit condition against the current public family vocabulary.

It records explicit judgement, comment, macro-category, family, colour mode, finish, complete graph/traits/palette, seed, generator/provenance hashes, perceptual measurements, rejection/attempt information, environment and viewing duration as context only.

What does **not** yet exist is a 250-item result. The manuscript currently handles this correctly by describing the study as planned/ongoing production research. Do not insert rates, aesthetic claims, selector-benefit claims, or “validation” language before the ratings and held-out batch exist.

The selector audit remains a separate future condition. Do not conflate the balanced generator audit with public-selector performance.

## 10. Style/register audit

### Strong passages that should survive

- “The object remained copyable while scarcity moved into a registry.”
- “A large formal possibility space had become perceptually small.”
- “per-pixel activity is cheap complexity.”
- “TSDNE does not refuse judgement. It refuses the conversion of visitor behaviour into an involuntary source of aesthetic training data.”
- “What if relevance is not estimated at all?”
- “The work therefore places scarcity in the wrong place.”
- “The legal position is deliberately less romantic than the interface gesture.”
- “A procedural system is also not without a corpus simply because it has no training dataset. Its corpus is less conveniently hidden.”
- “The dice are loaded.”
- “The shader can be reconstructed. The visitor should not have to be.”

These sentences do the characteristic Laurenzo work: a concrete system operation becomes a contradiction and then a political claim.

### Passages to reduce

The manuscript occasionally falls into machine-symmetrical enumeration:

> Visitor behaviour does not become preference. Difference does not become recommendation. Provenance does not become ownership. Reproducibility does not become collectibility. Memory does not become retrieval. Abundance does not become a catalogue.

The formulation is conceptually useful and can remain once, but the manuscript should not repeatedly restage the same six-item cadence in abstract/conclusion/section transitions.

Similarly, “no account, no profile, no model, no token, no certificate” is appropriate because Section 7 immediately criticises the list itself. Elsewhere, convert feature inventories into prose unless the inventory has analytical force.

### External-commentator register to remove

Phrases such as “This distinction matters because...” and “There is an important consequence here...” are serviceable but generic. Replace when possible with a more direct argumentative turn.

The manuscript should not sound as though another scholar is explaining what Laurenzo's work means. Use first person where a production decision or responsibility is actually the author's.

## 11. Art versus HCI balance

The current manuscript is already much less HCI-determined than the old plan, but the strongest references in Sections 1–3 are still recommendation/refusal/crypto/software sources.

Adding Gonzalez-Torres will correct an important imbalance because his work addresses:

- taking;
- disappearance/depletion;
- endless supply;
- reproducibility;
- public participation;
- circulation;
- singularity without simple material uniqueness.

Do **not** add a broad parade of relational-aesthetics artists or Tino Sehgal simply to create an “art” literature section. Add an artwork only when its material operation changes how TSDNE's apparatus can be understood.

## 12. Runtime-model argument: current scale is correct

The manuscript does not over-centre “no LLM”. It appears where the title and production history require it and returns in “The loaded dice” to reject purity rhetoric.

Keep this scale. Do not restore an AI-centric title, abstract, or section architecture.

## 13. Paper count: one paper remains correct

No second paper should be drafted from the same material now.

A second methods paper becomes justified only if the curation/selector programme later yields an independent result: for example, a reproducible artist-in-the-loop audit method, substantive evidence of selector bias, or a validated revision protocol. Until then, curation is production evidence for the main practice-led article.

## 14. Target venue

*Digital Creativity* remains the strongest first target. Its current official scope explicitly includes:

- the practical and theoretical aspects of making/using digital media in creative disciplines;
- relationships between practice, research, and technology;
- design and making of digital artefacts;
- interaction between digital media and audiences/publics;
- theoretical concepts.

The manuscript fits this intersection better than a narrowly technical venue. JSTA remains the strongest fallback.

1. Rewrite the opening from the shader encounter outward.
2. Add a precise Gonzalez-Torres comparison to the scarcity/circulation argument.
3. Remove Benjamin for now.
4. Source the “This Person Does Not Exist” title genealogy precisely and briefly.
5. Narrow the trace/privacy impossibility claim.
6. Reduce repeated parallel enumerations and generic transition phrases.
7. Preserve all empirical visitor questions as questions.
8. Keep the 250-item curation study prospective until data exist.

## Current judgement

**Major revision, but on the right manuscript.** The conceptual centre no longer needs replacement. The next pass should make the prose more authorial, the art genealogy more exact, and the remaining claims harder to misread than the implementation permits.
