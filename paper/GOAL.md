# TSDNE journal-paper goal

**Status date:** 7 August 2026  
**Primary target:** *Digital Creativity*  
**Fallback:** *Journal of Science and Technology of the Arts* (JSTA)  
**Publication form:** one substantial practice-led journal article

## Goal

Produce one strong journal article in which the realised artwork, its production history, its technical architecture, and its political argument are the same object of analysis.

The paper must not be assembled backwards from an available literature or from a novelty claim. It must begin from the artwork and from the contradictions that its apparatus produces.

The current working title is:

> **One Object, No Return: Scarcity, Refusal, and Provenance without Title in *This Shader Does Not Exist***

The title remains revisable after the manuscript has been read as a whole.

## Central thesis

TSDNE does not abolish scarcity, memory, provenance, or selection. It changes where they occur.

- scarcity moves from the object to the encounter;
- memory moves from the recoverable object to a lossy perceptual trace;
- provenance moves from title to computational relation;
- selection moves from behavioural extraction to the authored generative system.

The resulting architecture makes a digital object freely reproducible while making its encounter temporarily scarce; identifies an executable object without issuing ownership; remembers that an encounter occupied a perceptual region without preserving a route back to it; and produces difference without estimating what a visitor is likely to want.

This relocation, rather than any isolated shader-generation technique, is the paper's primary contribution.

## Four arguments that must survive every revision

### 1. Scarcity is relocated, not removed

TSDNE refuses proprietary, collectible, and exclusive scarcity. It nevertheless produces scarcity at the level of access: one object is available, **another** withdraws it from the interface, and there is no retrieval history.

The contradiction is deliberate:

> the source is non-scarce; the encounter is scarce.

The paper must therefore avoid claims of "singularity without scarcity" and instead analyse **scarcity of access without scarcity of possession**.

### 2. Difference is produced without inference

The system does not turn **another** into dislike, duration into engagement, return into preference, or any other visitor action into a behavioural model. The same action may be motivated by attraction, boredom, curiosity, testing, accident, or the desire to relinquish something valued. TSDNE leaves that ambiguity unresolved.

The paper may ask whether a personally meaningful relation can emerge without personalisation. It must not claim that such meaning has been empirically demonstrated unless an encounter study later establishes it.

The sharper implemented claim is **difference without inference**, and the sharper comparison with recommender systems is **serendipity without relevance optimisation**.

### 3. Provenance is retained while title is refused

The work contains at least two forms of provenance:

- **computational provenance:** seed, program, source, hashes, generator version, and technical manifest;
- **cultural/formal provenance:** explicit histories of visual and computational form, including abstract film, geometric abstraction, procedural mathematics, computer graphics, print/reprographic practices, and physical or biological simulation.

The paper must argue for **legibility rather than purity**. A procedural runtime without a learned corpus is not culturally source-less. Its debts can, however, be made more explicit and inspectable.

The central formulation remains:

> **TSDNE retains provenance but abolishes title.**

> **The shader can be identified, copied, and reconstructed, but not claimed.**

### 4. Memory is asymmetrical

TSDNE is not literally without archives. Git preserves software. Export preserves a chosen shader. Deterministic code can reproduce programs. Internal provenance identifies executable objects. The local anti-repetition system also remembers perceptual occupation.

What the artwork refuses is visitor retrieval through the encounter.

The paper should therefore describe an **encounter-level anti-archive** or a deliberately asymmetrical memory rather than a metaphysical disappearance. The work performs loss in a medium whose ordinary technical strength is exact reproduction.

## Production argument

The expansion from twelve to thirty-nine structural families, together with independent colour and finish axes, is evidence rather than a technical-priority claim.

The production history supports a specific proposition:

> Refusing visitor profiling does not remove selection. It relocates selection from surveillance of the visitor to curation of the generative system.

The private 250-item study must therefore distinguish:

1. a **balanced generator audit**, which samples the authored grammar independently of the public selector;
2. a **selector audit**, which examines what the public selection procedure chooses from the candidate pools it actually generates.

Development observations may guide revision. A held-out batch must be used before making any claim of post-revision improvement.

## Runtime-model position

The absence of a language model from the public runtime is relevant but secondary.

The durable argument is not "AI bad" or "procedural good". It is that generativity is not synonymous with foundation-model inference, and that the political consequences of a computational artwork depend on where computation is placed, what it is allowed to know, and what relations it produces.

AI-assisted software and documentation development must remain explicitly disclosed. This prevents the runtime architecture from being misrepresented as tool purity.

## Intended manuscript structure

1. **Introduction: one object, no return**  
   Computational abundance and the problem of how abundance is organised. State the relocation thesis.

2. **A shader appears**  
   Describe the artwork and production apparatus before abstracting it. Explain the 39-family grammar, structure/colour/finish separation, local candidate search, anti-repetition traces, and production curation.

3. **Difference without inference**  
   Recommender systems, personalisation, data minimisation, serendipity, ambiguous action, and designed refusal. Treat "personal without personalisation" as a question rather than a result.

4. **Scarcity in the wrong place**  
   Interface scarcity, reproducible objects, digital property, tokens, provenance markets, and the distinction between scarcity of access and scarcity of possession.

5. **Provenance without title**  
   Computational and cultural provenance, hashes without certificates, executable circulation, media appropriation, explicitation, free software, and the user-programmer continuum.

6. **Memory without retrieval**  
   Encounter-level anti-archive, deterministic reproducibility, local perceptual traces, Chun, Kirschenbaum, and performed disappearance.

7. **The loaded dice**  
   Authored selection, infrastructure, copyright limits, AI-assisted development, perceptual metrics, unresolved phenomenology, and conclusion.

## Claims the manuscript may make now

- TSDNE presents one generated shader at a time.
- replacement removes the previous shader from interface retrieval.
- public visitor actions are not converted into preference labels.
- persistent local memory contains non-reconstructive perceptual traces rather than shaders, seeds, source, or visitor profiles.
- executable source may be taken and reproduced outside the artwork without exclusive title.
- technical provenance is retained without a public ownership registry or certificate.
- the public runtime uses authored procedural generation rather than a learned generative model.
- the current generator contains thirty-nine structural families with independent colour and finish stages.
- the production process has already revised the generator in response to visible stylistic convergence.

## Claims requiring evidence before use

- that visitors become attached to a shader;
- that **another** causes regret or responsibility;
- that the encounter is experienced as personally meaningful;
- that the current generator sustains perceptual heterogeneity over long sessions;
- that the selector improves artistic quality;
- that the anti-repetition system measurably improves the encounter;
- that the system is clinically safe for photosensitive users;
- that every shader family and export works across browsers/drivers;
- that the absence of a model lowers energy use or produces an ethically superior system.

## Evidence programme

Before submission where feasible:

1. close the current candidate-pool sampling bias;
2. make the memory-clear operation clear all anti-repetition state it claims to clear;
3. recreate the private curation instrument from the current 39-family generator;
4. implement the balanced generator audit and separate selector audit;
5. complete the artist's 250-item exploratory review;
6. revise weak families/parameter regions/selector weights only after the exploratory batch;
7. evaluate revisions on a held-out batch;
8. run real WebGL2 compile/render validation across the grammar and representative colour/finish combinations;
9. test major desktop/mobile browser paths and all export targets;
10. preserve stills, videos, source versions, and analysis artefacts as paper evidence;
11. perform external accessibility/photosensitive-content review before claiming release readiness;
12. read every source cited in the final manuscript in the cited form.

## Paper-count decision

Do **not** split the current material into two papers.

The interaction architecture, anti-repetition mechanism, selector, executable export, provenance, and visual generator matter because they instantiate the same artistic and political proposition. Separating them now would produce a weaker art-theory article and a weak technical methods article.

A second paper becomes justified only if the generator/selector study yields an independent result: for example, a reproducible artist-in-the-loop evaluation method, a substantial selector-bias result, or a validated procedural-generator revision protocol. If that result does not emerge, the study remains production evidence for this article.

## Target length and venue fit

Draft for approximately **6,500–7,000 words**, excluding references, with figures and supplementary repository material.

*Digital Creativity* is the first target because the paper joins creative practice, digital artefact design, interaction, technical implementation, and critical/theoretical argument. JSTA is the strongest fallback because it explicitly accommodates practice-based artistic research and a longer article format.

## Completion rule

The manuscript is not complete because it is fluent. It is complete when:

- its central claims follow from the artwork and evidence;
- every factual citation is verified;
- every theoretical source has an actual job in the argument;
- the code and manuscript describe the same system;
- no empirical visitor experience is invented;
- the paper survives an adversarial claim audit;
- the prose has been rewritten out of generic machine-academic register;
- the final text reads as a continuation of Laurenzo's existing practice and writing rather than as an external interpretation of it.
