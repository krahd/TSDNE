## Digital memory, persistence, and materiality

### Wendy Hui Kyong Chun

**Reference:** Chun, Wendy Hui Kyong. 2008. “The Enduring Ephemeral, or the Future Is a Memory.” *Critical Inquiry* 35 (1): 148–171. https://doi.org/10.1086/595632.

**Status:** M, P.

**Verified from:** University of Chicago Press record/full-text endpoint.

**Use in paper:** Section “Memory without retrieval.” Digital media's relation between apparent ephemerality and persistent/regenerative memory. Use to complicate, not validate, TSDNE's encounter-level disappearance.

**Claim boundary:** Do not attribute TSDNE's phrase “remembers enough not to repeat, but not enough to restore” to Chun. It is a project formulation that must be situated in, not substituted for, the literature.

### Matthew G. Kirschenbaum

**Reference:** Kirschenbaum, Matthew G. 2008. *Mechanisms: New Media and the Forensic Imagination*. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press.

**Status:** M, P.

**Use in paper:** Distinguish interface/formal behaviour from the persistence and material specificity of computational storage. Particularly relevant to the difference between a shader becoming inaccessible through TSDNE and the continued reproducibility of its program.

**Claim boundary:** Do not collapse Kirschenbaum's formal/forensic materiality distinction into a simple “software persists” slogan.

## Personalisation, recommendation, and data minimisation

### Asia J. Biega, Peter Potash, Hal Daumé III, Fernando Diaz, Michèle Finck

**Reference:** Biega, Asia J., Peter Potash, Hal Daumé III, Fernando Diaz, and Michèle Finck. 2020. “Operationalizing the Legal Principle of Data Minimization for Personalization.” In *Proceedings of the 43rd International ACM SIGIR Conference on Research and Development in Information Retrieval*, 399–408. https://doi.org/10.1145/3397271.3401034.

**Status:** M, P.

**Use in paper:** Establish that privacy-minded recommender research can preserve personalisation as the objective while asking how little personal data it requires. TSDNE's implemented difference is not a better minimisation algorithm: it refuses to estimate visitor relevance at all.

**Claim boundary:** Do not imply the authors argue against personalisation. Their paper investigates data minimisation within personalisation.

### Reza Jafari Ziarani and Reza Ravanmehr

**Reference:** Ziarani, Reza Jafari, and Reza Ravanmehr. 2021. “Serendipity in Recommender Systems: A Systematic Literature Review.” *Journal of Computer Science and Technology* 36 (2): 375–396. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11390-020-0135-9.

**Status:** M, P.

**Use in paper:** Establish the occupied research space around recommender-system serendipity and its recurrent relation to relevance, novelty, and unexpectedness.

### Denis Kotkov, Alan Medlar, and Dorota Glowacka

**Reference:** Kotkov, Denis, Alan Medlar, and Dorota Glowacka. 2023. “Rethinking Serendipity in Recommender Systems.” In *Proceedings of the 2023 Conference on Human Information Interaction and Retrieval*, 383–387. https://doi.org/10.1145/3576840.3578310.

**Status:** M, P.

**Use in paper:** Support the claim that recommender research itself recognises that operational serendipity can be narrower than broader lived/social senses of serendipity. TSDNE can then be framed as **serendipity without relevance optimisation**, without pretending to invent serendipity.

## Refusal and data practice

### Jonathan Zong and J. Nathan Matias

**Reference:** Zong, Jonathan, and J. Nathan Matias. 2024. “Data Refusal from Below: A Framework for Understanding, Evaluating, and Envisioning Refusal as Design.” *ACM Journal on Responsible Computing* 1 (1): 1–23. https://doi.org/10.1145/3630107.

**Status:** M, P.

**Use in paper:** Clarify that refusal can reshape socio-technical systems and can be understood as design.

**Critical boundary:** Their framework is explicitly written from the standpoint of people and communities refusing institutions and data collectors. TSDNE is an authored artwork that refuses to perform certain conversions on its visitors. The paper must state this difference and must not appropriate “refusal from below” as the artwork's own political position.

### Patricia Garcia, Tonia Sutherland, Niloufar Salehi, Marika Cifor, and Anubha Singh

**Reference:** Garcia, Patricia, Tonia Sutherland, Niloufar Salehi, Marika Cifor, and Anubha Singh. 2022. “No! Re-imagining Data Practices Through the Lens of Critical Refusal.” *Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction* 6 (CSCW2), Article 315: 1–20. https://doi.org/10.1145/3557997.

**Status:** M, P.

**Use in paper:** Critical refusal as an active practice that investigates unequal power and generates alternative data practices. Useful for distinguishing refusal from mere subtraction or feature absence.

**Critical boundary:** Keep the feminist-data-practice genealogy and political stakes explicit. TSDNE can be put into conversation with the concept; it should not claim equivalence with the cases studied by Garcia et al.

## Provenance, tokens, and artificial scarcity

### Massimo Franceschet et al.

**Reference:** Franceschet, Massimo, Giovanni Colavizza, T’Ai Smith, Blake Finucane, Martin Lukas Ostachowski, Sergio Scalet, Jonathan Perkins, James Morgan, and Sebastian Hernández. 2021. “Crypto Art: A Decentralized View.” *Leonardo* 54 (4): 402–405. https://doi.org/10.1162/leon_a_02003.

**Status:** M, P.

**Use in paper:** Establish crypto art as a concrete counter-model in which cryptographic registration and provenance are connected to limited-edition digital art and tradable tokens. TSDNE's contrast is not “hashing versus no hashing”; it is what social operation follows identification.

### Rachel O'Dwyer

**Reference:** O'Dwyer, Rachel. 2023. *Tokens: The Future of Money in the Age of the Platform*. London: Verso. Hardback ISBN 978-1-83976-834-7.

**Status:** M, P.

**Use in paper:** Situate tokens as programmable/platform forms that join value, access, ownership, identity, governance, and surveillance. Useful for preventing a narrowly NFT-specific account of title and programmable scarcity.

## Free software, circulation, appropriation

### Christopher M. Kelty

**Reference:** Kelty, Christopher M. 2008. *Two Bits: The Cultural Significance of Free Software*. Durham, NC: Duke University Press. https://doi.org/10.1215/9780822389002.

**Status:** M, P.

**Use in paper:** Free software as a reorganisation of the power to create, circulate, modify, and maintain technical infrastructures; relevant to executable continuation and to the politics of source availability.

**Claim boundary:** Do not call TSDNE a “recursive public” unless the paper actually establishes the organisational/infrastructural conditions Kelty associates with that term.

### Lawrence Lessig

**Reference:** Lessig, Lawrence. 2004. *Free Culture: How Big Media Uses Technology and the Law to Lock Down Culture and Control Creativity*. New York: Penguin Press.

**Status:** M. Relevant passage reading required before use.

**Use in paper:** Possible background for free cultural circulation and legal enclosure. Not necessary if Kelty and Laurenzo's prior framework do the argumentative work more precisely.

### Lewis Hyde

**Reference:** Hyde, Lewis. *The Gift: How the Creative Spirit Transforms the World*. Use only after final edition is selected and read.

**Status:** lead only.

**Use in paper:** Possible discussion of circulation, but likely unnecessary. Avoid romanticising TSDNE as a gift economy when the stronger claim concerns executable continuation and non-exclusive possession.

## Laurenzo's prior framework

### Media appropriation and explicitation

**Reference:** Laurenzo, Tomas. 2016. “Media Appropriation and Explicitation.” *Journal of Science and Technology of the Arts* 8 (2): 27–36. https://doi.org/10.7559/citarj.v8i2.181.

**Status:** M, P; author source.

**Use in paper:** The export of executable source and the authored generator should be related to media appropriation and explicitation: technical knowledge may become part of the artistic material rather than remain hidden behind an interface.

### Politics of HCI and the User–Programmer Continuum

**Reference:** Laurenzo, Tomas. 2017. “Politics of HCI and the User–Programmer Continuum.” In *ISEA2017: 23rd International Symposium on Electronic Art* proceedings.

**Status:** P; final proceedings pagination/metadata to verify before final bibliography.

**Use in paper:** The political consequences of assigning stable roles of user/programmer; **take the code** makes a movement along this continuum materially possible without requiring every visitor to programme.

### 5500: Performance, Control, and Politics

**Reference:** Laurenzo, Tomas. 2016. “5500: Performance, Control, and Politics.” In *Proceedings of the International Conference on New Interfaces for Musical Expression*, 36–40. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1176058.

**Status:** M, R; author source.

**Use in paper:** Primarily a writing/methodological precedent rather than necessary self-citation. If cited, use only where the paper discusses partial control and apparatuses that act back on users.

## Sources that should probably *not* carry the main argument

- Zuboff as a universal shorthand for all platform extraction;
- Pariser as a sufficient account of recommender politics;
- generic NFT criticism without technical/social specificity;
- generic “AI ethics” surveys;
- novelty-search papers as though TSDNE contributes a new optimisation algorithm;
- broad generative-art histories used only to demonstrate that generative art exists.

They may be background leads, but the final bibliography should be selective and argumentative.
